What to Watch Out For in the Finglas Driving Test

1. Tight estates & constant hazards

Finglas is heavy on narrow residential roads with parked cars, bends, and kids crossing. You're expected to read priority correctly and slow early. Charging on or being unsure gets marked.

2. Observation at junctions (big fail area)

There are loads of minor junctions with limited visibility. Full stop, clear head movement, edge out only when safe. Rolling stops and lazy checks are common fail reasons.

3. Speed control on residential roads

Speed limits are low and unforgiving. Creeping above the limit — especially downhill or after turns — racks up faults quickly.

4. Manoeuvres in awkward spaces

Reverse around a corner and turnabout are very likely and often tighter than you expect. Kerbing, poor steering control, or missing blind-spot checks will cost you.

5. Traffic flow & hesitation

Traffic can be stop-start. Over-hesitating at junctions or roundabouts, or blocking traffic unnecessarily, gets marked down. You need to be safe and decisive.

6. Lane positioning on wider roads

Some routes move briefly onto wider roads where poor lane discipline shows immediately. Signal early, position early, and don't drift.

Bottom line

Finglas catches people out on observation, speed discipline, and estate driving. It's not about confidence — it's about calm control and constant scanning. Sloppy habits get exposed fast.
