Cavan Free Driving Test Route

A turn-by-turn practice route from the Cavan test centre. Follow it locally before test day.

From Cavan Test Centre leave Meadow Park and at the end of the road turn left onto the Dublin Road.

Continue for about half a kilometre and go straight through the roundabout, staying on the Dublin Road.

Take the next right into Lough View.

Take the 1st right into Laragh Crescent and pull in for a stop.

Move off from Laragh Crescent and turn right onto the Cavan Town Centre Eastern Access Road.

At the roundabout turn left onto the Dublin Road.

At the next roundabout take a slight left onto the Cavan Bypass.

Stay on the Cavan Bypass for about 8 km.

Turn right and then right again onto Main Street and pull in for a stop.

Pull away from Main Street and continue straight as the road becomes Derrygarra Upper Road.

At the end of the road turn left back onto the Cavan Bypass.

Take the 1st right onto Cathedral Road.

Take the slight left into Drumgola Lane and follow it around.

Turn left back onto Cathedral Road.

At the roundabout turn right, staying on Cathedral Road.

Go straight into Manager's Road and pull in for a stop.

Set off from Manager's Road and continue straight as it becomes Drumalee Road.

At the end of the road turn right onto Ardkeen Road.

Take the 1st right into Ardkeen Court and stop.

Move off and follow Ardkeen Court around to the right and then left.

Turn left back onto Ardkeen Road.

Take the next right onto Killynebber Road.

At the fork bear slight right onto Reservoir Road.

Turn left and then right onto the Cavan Bypass.

At the roundabout turn left onto the Dublin Road and exit left to stay on it.

Take the next left, still on the Dublin Road.

For the extended practice loop, leave Meadow Park again and turn left onto the Dublin Road.

Go straight through the roundabout and continue as the road becomes College Street.

Turn left onto Wolfe Tone Street and carry straight on through Glenside Road and Swellan Road.

Go straight onto Farnham Road.

Take the 1st right onto Farnham-Gartbrattan Road.

Continue straight onto Derredis-Farnham Road.

Make your way back towards the Dublin Road.

Turn right into Meadow Park to arrive back at Cavan Test Centre.

Cavan Driving Test Routes – What to Expect, Common Mistakes & Local Tips

If you're searching for Cavan driving test routes, you're probably trying to figure out where examiners usually bring learners, which junctions catch people out, and how to avoid easy fails.

This page is built specifically for the Cavan RSA driving test centre. It focuses on real local test patterns around Cavan town, Swellan, Dublin Road approaches, the N3 corridor, Ballinagh Road, and surrounding residential estates — not generic advice.

Updated July 2026 based on learner feedback and local route observations.

Below is a practical breakdown of the roads that appear again and again on Cavan test routes, the pressure points learners struggle with most, and what actually causes people to fail here.

About the Cavan Driving Test Centre

The Cavan test centre serves learner drivers from Cavan town, Ballyhaise, Ballinagh, Virginia outskirts, Butlersbridge, and surrounding rural areas of County Cavan.

The driving environment is mixed:

  • Urban traffic around Cavan town centre
  • Residential estates with narrow internal roads
  • National primary routes such as the N3
  • Regional connector roads
  • Retail park and business park access roads
  • Rural approach roads with higher speed limits

Cavan is not just a quiet rural test. Most routes combine town junctions, national road joins, estate manoeuvres, retail traffic and rural speed transitions.

How Test Routes Work at This Centre

There are no fixed driving test routes in Cavan.

Examiners reuse connected road networks and looping patterns around the town and its approaches.

Typical route structure:

  • Leaving the test centre into residential streets
  • Passing through town centre traffic zones
  • Entering estates for manoeuvres
  • Transitioning onto main roads such as the N3 or Dublin Road
  • Looping back via alternate residential connectors

Because Cavan is compact, examiners often reuse the same roads from different directions. This tests consistency and observation rather than memorisation.

Learning how traffic flows through Cavan town and its approach roads matters far more than remembering turn sequences.

Core Areas Examiners Frequently Use

While routes vary daily, learners are regularly brought through:

  • Dublin Road (N3) corridor
  • Ballinagh Road approaches
  • Swellan Road area
  • Farnham Road and town centre network
  • Butlersbridge direction roads
  • Retail park access roads
  • Virginia Road connectors
  • Residential estates around Drumalee, Swellan, Killynebber, Oakdene, Ashgrove, and surrounding housing developments

These areas form the backbone of most Cavan test routes.

Roundabout Behaviour at This Centre

Roundabouts are a common fail point in Cavan.

Larger / multi-lane roundabouts

Found near N3 junctions, retail parks and main town entry points.

Examiners watch for:

  • Early lane positioning
  • Correct approach lane choice
  • Mirror checks before lane changes
  • Staying in lane through the roundabout
  • Clear exit signalling

Common mistakes:

  • Late lane changes
  • Cutting across lanes
  • Hesitating unnecessarily at busy entries
  • Forgetting to signal off

Smaller residential roundabouts

Found inside estates.

These test:

  • Observation discipline
  • Yield judgement
  • Smooth approach speed

Learners often brake too hard or enter without fully checking right.

Junction Types That Cause Fails

Hidden residential junctions

Common in older Cavan estates. Learners fail here due to:

  • Rolling stops
  • Weak left-right scanning
  • Pulling out when parked cars block visibility

Fast approach junctions onto main roads

Seen when joining the N3 or Dublin Road. These test gap judgement.

Hesitation = fault. Unsafe entry = immediate fail.

Estate exits

Common around Drumalee and Swellan estates. Drivers often creep too far forward or forget blind spot checks.

Offset T-junctions

Present in older town layouts. Learners misjudge alignment and right-of-way.

Junctions after bends

Found on rural connectors approaching town. Late braking and poor positioning cause repeated faults.

Speed Limit Traps

Speed control is one of the most common fail reasons in Cavan. Typical traps include:

  • N3 town entry sections where speed drops quickly
  • Wide town roads that feel faster than posted limits
  • Residential estate entrances
  • School zones near town centre
  • Rural roads that suddenly enter 50 km/h zones

Why learners fail: they drive based on road width instead of signage. Examiners expect early braking and immediate compliance.

Common Mistakes at This Centre

Repeated faults seen on Cavan test reports include:

  • Weak mirror checks before pulling out
  • Late or missing indicators
  • Hesitation joining main roads
  • Poor positioning on narrow town streets
  • Forgetting blind spot checks
  • Over-braking at roundabouts
  • Speed creeping above limits
  • Poor observation at estate exits
  • Rushing manoeuvres

Most learners fail through small repeated mistakes, not one dramatic error.

Examiner Behaviour Patterns (Local Feedback)

Learners consistently report that Cavan examiners:

  • Focus heavily on observation at junctions
  • Watch mirror–signal routines closely
  • Are strict on speed compliance near town entrances
  • Expect confident but safe gap selection
  • Penalise repeated hesitation
  • Value smooth, predictable driving

They want steady control, not aggressive or overly cautious behaviour.

High-Failure Locations / Hotspots

Based on repeated learner feedback:

  • Dublin Road (N3) junctions
  • Ballinagh Road intersections
  • Retail park roundabouts
  • Swellan Road junction areas
  • Residential estate exits
  • Town centre traffic-light junctions

These spots combine traffic pressure, speed transitions and limited visibility.

Test Day Flow at This Centre

Leaving the centre

You usually exit into residential traffic. Early mirror checks and correct positioning matter immediately.

Early test pressure points

Town centre junctions and roundabouts appear quickly. Many learners lose early marks here.

Mid-test environment

Expect estate driving for manoeuvres — reverse around corner, turnabout or hill start — combined with tight observation requirements.

Final section

Routes often return via main town roads. Learners commonly relax here and lose marks on signalling or speed control.

Local Preparation Tips

  • Practise N3 and Dublin Road entry/exit junctions repeatedly
  • Drive Cavan town centre during school traffic hours
  • Repeat manoeuvres inside Drumalee and Swellan estates
  • Practise rural-to-town speed transitions
  • Drill mirror–signal routines
  • Train smooth braking before junctions

If you can handle Cavan traffic during peak periods, the test becomes far easier.

FAQ – Cavan Driving Test

How long is the driving test here?

Usually 35–40 minutes, including vehicle checks and manoeuvres.

Are routes fixed?

No. Cavan uses multiple test routes that reuse the same core road networks.

Can I practise realistic routes?

Yes. You can practise realistic Cavan route patterns that reflect real examiner behaviour and test structure.

Is this centre considered hard?

Cavan is considered moderate difficulty due to national road joins, town junctions and rural-to-urban transitions.

What is the best way to prepare?

Drive the area repeatedly. Practise town centre junctions, estate exits, roundabouts and speed changes until everything feels routine.

What is the Cavan pass rate and waiting time?
