Drogheda is a large, busy town in County Louth with a genuinely varied road network — urban streets, residential estates, approach roads, national routes, and business parks all within a short radius of Southgate. That mix is what makes this centre worth preparing for carefully.
About the Drogheda Southgate RSA Test Centre
The Drogheda test centre at Southgate Shopping Centre is a newly opened RSA facility, confirmed in April 2026 after a long campaign to restore driving test access to Drogheda. Learners from the area had previously been forced to travel to Dundalk for their tests, so demand here is high from day one.
The centre serves Drogheda town and a wide surrounding catchment including South Louth and East Meath. That means learners from Laytown, Bettystown, Duleek, Julianstown, Termonfeckin, and Collon will all be using this test centre.
The driving environment around the Southgate centre includes:
Urban town centre roads with traffic lights, bus lanes, and pedestrian crossings
Established residential estates on the north and west sides of town
National routes including the R132 (old N1) Dublin Road and Donore Road
Business and retail parks along the approach roads
Newer housing developments to the north around Ballymakenny Road
Riverside areas near the Boyne with narrower, older road layouts
How Test Routes Work at This Centre
There are no fixed, published routes. The RSA does not hand out a list of turns and tell examiners to follow it. What happens in practice is that examiners use a network of roads they know well and rotate through combinations of them depending on traffic, time of day, and how the test is going.
Because this is a brand new centre, examiners will be establishing their patterns over the first months of operation. What learner feedback already suggests — and what the road geography makes almost certain — is that tests will loop through a combination of town centre roads, residential estates, and the main approach routes into Drogheda.
The RSA’s own guidance states that the driving tester can take you anywhere within a 5km radius of the test centre. For Drogheda Southgate, that 5km radius covers a significant portion of the town and stretches out toward the N1/M1 approaches, the Meath border to the south, and the Baltray road to the northeast.
What matters is not memorising a specific route. What matters is getting comfortable with the types of roads, junctions, and roundabouts that appear repeatedly across that area.

Roundabout Behaviour at This Centre
Larger Roundabouts
The Rosehall Roundabout on the R132 north is the largest roundabout learners are likely to encounter. It has multiple exits, handles volume traffic, and has approach speeds that feel faster than a typical town road. Examiners watch closely here for lane positioning on approach, whether you’ve selected the correct lane early, and whether you yield properly to traffic already on the roundabout.
The Ballymakenny Road Roundabout is newer and the road markings and signage are cleaner, but learners who haven’t driven that area before can be caught out by the approach angle. The road feeds in from a stretch that feels quite open, and the instinct can be to carry too much speed onto the roundabout.
Smaller Residential Roundabouts
Mini roundabouts appear throughout Drogheda’s newer estates. These are the ones that trip people up most often. The instinct is to treat them like a give-way line and barely slow down. Examiners here want to see you acknowledge the roundabout, look right for oncoming traffic, and position the car correctly before proceeding.
Common roundabout mistakes:
Not checking right before entering a mini roundabout
Cutting across the central island on tight roundabouts
Indicating incorrectly — or not at all — when exiting
Stalling or hesitating mid-roundabout under pressure
Choosing the wrong lane at multi-exit roundabouts
Junction Types That Cause Fails
Drogheda’s road network has a mixture of well-signed main road junctions and tighter residential exits where visibility is limited. The latter are where most observation faults happen.
Watch out for:
Estate exits onto the Rathmullan Road and Ballymakenny Road — parked cars near the junction mouth reduce your sightline, you need to creep forward and look before committing
T-junctions off the old town roads near the Boyne — these are older layouts with no clear road markings, and examiners watch whether you treat them with appropriate caution
Junctions on approach to the town centre where the road bends slightly before the give-way line — learners who don’t open their eyes to what’s ahead get caught out
Offset crossroads — particularly between estate roads on the Newfoundwell Road side — where learners assume a clear path and miss approaching traffic
Side roads feeding onto the R132 — the speed differential between your car turning in and through traffic on the main road is significant; pull out only when genuinely clear
Speed Limit Traps in the Drogheda Test Area
Speed limit transitions are one of the most common sources of minor faults on Irish tests — and Drogheda has several zones where learners consistently misjudge this.
Key zones to know:
The R132 approaching town from the south — the limit drops from 80km/h to 60km/h then to 50km/h within a short stretch. Learners often carry 80km/h into a 60km/h zone before they’ve spotted the sign.
Ballymakenny Road — currently a 50km/h zone but with stretches that feel like open road due to the newer layout. The absence of dense housing on sections tricks learners into driving faster than the limit allows.
Newfoundwell Road and the residential connectors off it — all 50km/h, but the road is wide enough in places that learners unconsciously push past that.
Near schools on Rathmullan Road and along the Ballymakenny corridor — school zone 30km/h limits apply at certain times. These are strictly marked but easy to miss if you’re not reading the road signs.
Bull Ring and town centre — a 30km/h zone. If you come through the town on a test, you need to be well under 30. Examiners notice if you’re pushing 40 through here.
Common Mistakes at This Centre
Because this is a new centre, specific local failure data isn’t yet published by the RSA. What we can draw on is learner experience from the previous Drogheda centre (which had a 62% pass rate), combined with what the road geography here makes predictable:
Observation at junctions — not creeping far enough forward to actually see before pulling out, or looking but not truly checking
Hesitation on roundabouts — stopping when it’s safe to go, or going when it isn’t. Either direction gets marked
Mirror checks that happen after the decision rather than before — a mechanical habit issue that examiners identify immediately
Poor positioning on wider roads — drifting left on the R132 or not taking a clear road position when turning right
Late signalling — indicating as you’re already turning rather than giving enough notice beforehand
Failing to anticipate pedestrian crossings in the town centre — Drogheda town has active pedestrian crossings, particularly on West Street and around the shopping areas
Examiner Behaviour Patterns
As this is a newly opened centre, examiners here have been drawn from nearby centres including Dundalk. Learner feedback from Dundalk indicates that examiners in the Louth area tend to be methodical and consistent — they follow the same marking criteria as any other RSA examiner in Ireland.
What learners consistently report from RSA tests generally: examiners give directions early enough to act on them, they do not engage in conversation during the test, and they are marking from the moment you move off. They are not trying to trip you up, but they are watching continuously.
What examiners in Louth-area centres tend to focus on: junction observation, speed compliance, and roundabout positioning. These are the three areas generating the most faults in this part of the country based on RSA data patterns.
One practical note: if an examiner gives you a direction you’re not sure about, do not hesitate or panic. Drive on safely, and if you genuinely cannot make the turn safely, they will redirect you. What they will not tolerate is rushing a manoeuvre because you feel pressured.
High-Failure Locations and Hotspots
Based on the road layout around Southgate and what learners from the previous Drogheda centre have reported, these are the areas most likely to generate faults:
Rosehall Roundabout (R132 North)
The volume and pace of traffic here catches learners by surprise. The approach road feels fast, the roundabout is large, and choosing the correct exit under pressure is harder than it looks. Practice this roundabout specifically.
Estate Exits onto Rathmullan Road
Narrow exit mouths with parked cars close to the junction line. You need to inch forward until you can genuinely see. Learners who give a cursory look from behind the give-way line and pull out are marked immediately.
R132 Speed Limit Transition (South Approach)
The 80-60-50 zone transition on the Dublin Road coming into town is a consistent source of speed-related faults. Know where each limit starts.
Town Centre Junctions (West Street / Laurence Street Area)
High pedestrian activity, traffic lights that cycle quickly, buses stopping, and cyclists. This environment demands full attention and smooth control. Learners who get here having already made a few faults tend to tighten up and make more.
Test Day Flow at This Centre
You’ll check in at the Southgate Shopping Centre location, beside the NDLS centre. Bring your learner permit and your vehicle. The examiner will come to you, do a brief check of your documentation, and then carry out the vehicle safety questions before you move off.
The first minute of the test is when most people feel the pressure. You’re moving off, probably from the car park or the immediate surrounding roads, and you’re acutely aware that the test has started. Breathe, check your mirrors, and drive as you’ve been trained. The examiner isn’t watching you any more carefully in the first minute than in the last.
Mid-test, you’ll likely be on a mix of estate roads and a main road or two. This is the longest section and where most learners settle. If you’ve made a fault, don’t dwell on it. The test continues and a single fault does not fail you. Multiple minor faults in the same category will.
The final section often involves a manoeuvre — a turnabout, a reverse, or a bay park. These are done in quieter residential or car park areas. Stay calm, take your time, and observe properly at every check.
The test is approximately 30 minutes long. You’ll be told the result immediately on return to the test centre.
Local Preparation Tips
Drive the Rosehall Roundabout and the Ballymakenny Road Roundabout until they feel routine. Both are almost certain to appear on your test in some form.
Practise estate exits with limited visibility — particularly on Rathmullan Road and the roads off Newfoundwell Road. Get into the habit of creeping and looking, not stopping and assuming.
Know your speed limits on the R132 approach to town. Drive that stretch multiple times and identify where each limit changes.
If possible, do a pre-test lesson with an instructor who knows the Southgate centre specifically. The first routes out of a new centre tend to be predictable for the first few months.
Tuesday to Thursday mornings are generally quieter for traffic in Drogheda. If you have a choice of test slot, these windows will give you lighter conditions to work with.
Drive through the town centre — West Street, Laurence Street, the quays area — in normal conditions so it doesn’t feel unfamiliar on test day.
Practise your manoeuvres in the Southgate car park or a nearby quiet area. Familiarity with the physical space around the test centre helps.

FAQ — Drogheda Southgate RSA Test Centre
How long is the driving test here?
Around 30 minutes of driving, plus the time at the start for documentation and vehicle safety questions. Budget about 40 minutes from start to result.
Are the routes fixed?
No. The RSA does not publish fixed routes. Examiners use a network of local roads and can vary them. The test can go anywhere within approximately 5km of the Southgate centre.
Can I practise realistic routes before my test?
Yes. The most effective preparation is to drive the roads around the centre — particularly the Rosehall Roundabout, Ballymakenny Road, Rathmullan Road, the R132 Dublin Road approach, and the residential estates between them. DriveFlow.ie provides structured Google Maps routes for Irish test centres to make this easier.
Is this centre considered hard?
It’s too early for published pass rate data from the RSA on this centre — the first official figures won’t appear until 2027. The previous Drogheda test centre had a 62% pass rate, which is reasonable by Irish standards. The road environment here is varied but not extreme. Learners who have genuinely prepared for the local roads should find it manageable.
What is the best way to prepare?
Drive the local roads regularly before your test. Focus on roundabouts, estate junction exits, and the speed limit zones on the approach roads. Do at least one pre-test lesson with an instructor who knows the Southgate area. Know your vehicle safety questions. And on the day, drive exactly as you have been practising — not faster, not more hesitantly.
