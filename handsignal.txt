1. Turning Left

What it means: “I’m turning left.”
How to do it:

Put your left arm straight out horizontally through the window.

Hand flat, palm facing forward.
Easy. Clean. Straight line.

2. Turning Right

What it means: “I’m turning right.”
How to do it:

Stick your right arm straight out, or

If you can’t use the right arm out the window (Irish cars are RHD), raise your left arm up, elbow bent, palm facing forward.

3. Slowing Down / Stopping

What it means: “I’m slowing or stopping.”
How to do it:

Put your right arm out and move it up and down like you’re patting a big dog.
Steady, clear movement — not frantic.

4. Overtaking / Passing

What it means: “I’m letting you know I’m overtaking.”
How to do it:

Extend your right arm out, make a forward sweeping motion.
You won’t use this often, but you must know it.

5. Cyclist Hand Signals

Testers sometimes mix car + cyclist questions.

Left turn: Left arm straight out.

Right turn: Right arm straight out.

Slowing: One hand out, palm down, moving up and down.

Exact same principles — simpler because cyclists don’t use windows.

6. How the Tester Checks You

They’ll either:

Ask you to describe a signal (“Show me how you’d signal slowing down”), or

Ask you to physically do them in the seat.

Do them confidently. Hesitation kills marks.

7. Quick Tip to Remember Them

Straight arm = direction.

Bent arm up = right turn (if you can’t use the right window).

Up-and-down = slowing.

Forward sweep = overtaking.

If you can picture these four moves, you’re sorted.
