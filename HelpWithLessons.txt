2. Before you ever sit in the driver’s seat

[ ] Know the Rules of the Road properly

Read the RSA Rules of the Road cover-to-cover at least once; it’s literally what both theory and practical are based on.
RSA
+1

Focus especially on:

Yellow warning signs and road markings (hatching, solid vs broken lines, bus lanes) – these get asked at the start of the test.
Reddit
+1

Right-of-way rules at junctions, roundabouts, and pedestrian crossings.

[ ] Understand how the test is actually marked

RSA marking guidelines:

Any Grade 3 fault = instant fail

4 of the same Grade 2 in one area = fail

Too many Grade 2s overall = fail.
RSA

Common fail reasons straight from the RSA: inadequate observation, poor road position, poor progress, bad mirror/signal use, ignoring signs/markings, wrong speed, right-of-way mistakes, and weak manoeuvres.
RSA

Build your whole training around not picking up Grade 2s in those areas.

That’s the boring prep, but anyone who skips it will leak marks everywhere.

3. How to get the most out of each EDT lesson

Here’s a repeatable template your users can follow every single lesson.

Before the lesson

[ ] Set 1–2 specific goals
Example: “Today I want to fix lane positioning at roundabouts and my clutch control starting on hills.”

[ ] Bring a notes app / notebook
Jot down faults and fixes immediately after.

During the lesson

Tell them to actively use the instructor, not just sit there:

[ ] Ask the instructor to talk like a tester at least some of the time: minimal talking, just directions and marking faults.

[ ] Ask “why?” constantly.
“Why was that a Grade 2?”, “What would have made that perfect?”
They need to understand the reason behind every correction – that’s how they stop repeating it.

[ ] Practice decision-making, not just steering.
Spend time on judging gaps at junctions/roundabouts, not just doing endless laps.
autoglass.ie
+1

After the lesson

[ ] Log 3 things done well + 3 things to fix next time.

[ ] Write a one-line rule for each mistake.
e.g. “Every time before braking, mirror first”, “Always check blind spot before moving off”.

If an instructor isn’t happy to do this kind of structured review, they’re not great.

4. Driving between lessons (this is where people actually pass or fail)

Almost every experienced instructor / Irish Reddit thread says the same: lessons alone aren’t enough. You need real miles.
Reddit
+1

[ ] Aim for at least 3–5 hours driving a week total, not just the 1 hour with the ADI.

[ ] Practise in your own car (if possible)

Always with a fully licensed driver over 2 years, insured, L-plates, etc.

Try to replicate lesson topics in your own car: same junctions, same roundabouts.

[ ] Focus practice on what people actually fail on

Use the RSA fail list like a training menu:
RSA
+1

Observation:

Look: mirrors → signal → blind spot check → move, every time.

Practise big head movements when checking mirrors and blind spots – testers need to see it.

Road position:

Centre of lane, correct lane on roundabouts, correct position on left/right turns.

Progress:

Not crawling everywhere. Irish testers will fail you for being too slow and holding up traffic if it’s safe to go.
Reddit
+1

Mirrors/signals:

Before: changing speed, changing direction, changing position.

Speed control:

Practice holding 48–52 in 50 zones, 58–62 in 60 zones where safe – not 40 everywhere.
autoglass.ie
+1

Right of way & traffic controls:

Signs, markings, yellow boxes, pedestrian crossings, filter lanes.

Build your checklist page so learners can tick off each of those with real practice examples, not just theory.

5. 4–2 weeks before the test: go into “test mode”

[ ] Do at least one full mock test with an instructor

Same length, same centre, minimal talking, full marking sheet. Learners on r/ireland and r/Dublin say this is one of the biggest game-changers.

Whether any pattern would cause a fail (e.g. 4 x “progress” Grade 2).

[ ] Drive the test area at the same time of day

7. Headspace / nerves (what actual learners say helped)

Pulled from multiple Irish Reddit threads:
Reddit
+3
Reddit
+3
Reddit
+3

[ ] Treat it like “just another drive”

It’s 30–40 minutes of the driving you should already be doing.

Expect to make tiny mistakes; testers aren’t looking for perfection, they’re looking for safe and consistent.

[ ] Be predictable, not heroic

No sudden lane changes, no panic braking.

Indicate, then follow through smoothly – don’t bottle it halfway through a manoeuvre.

[ ] Mirrors, mirrors, mirrors

People who passed often say: “I just kept checking mirrors and doing the basics.”

Build a habit of a quick mirror scan every 5–10 seconds and before any change.

[ ] Hesitation vs rushing

It’s better to wait a bit longer at a tricky roundabout than to launch yourself into a dodgy gap.

But if there’s a clear safe gap and you sit there forever, that’s a “lack of progress” mark – hence the practice on judging gaps beforehand.

8. Small “cheat codes” (all legal)

[ ] Cheap blind-spot mirrors

Irish instructors have pushed those little convex blind-spot mirrors (the €2 ones from Lidl/Aldi) as a huge help – wider field of view, better awareness when changing lanes/merging.
The Sun

Still must do proper shoulder checks – but they reduce surprises.

[ ] Drive in the test car as much as possible

If you’ll test in the instructor’s car, try to do the majority of your lessons in it.

If you’re using your own car, practise only in that car for at least a week before test: pedals, bite point, indicator feel, everything.

[ ] Have a “pre-drive ritual”

Quick mental checklist: Mirrors set → Seat & steering → Seatbelt → Handbrake → Clutch test → Breathe.

Repeating the same start-up routine kills nerves and prevents stupid early mistakes.
