Irish Driving Test Mirror Checks Explained

Mirror checks are one of the easiest ways to lose marks on the RSA driving test, and one of the easiest habits to fix before test day. Examiners are not looking for a robotic head swivel every five seconds. They are watching to see whether you actually use the information in your mirrors to make safe decisions. This guide breaks down exactly when to check, what your examiner wants to see, and the small habits that separate a confident learner from one who picks up faults on the report sheet.

Why mirror checks matter so much

On the RSA driving test report sheet, "observation" faults appear under multiple headings: moving off, changing lane, overtaking, turning, and stopping. If you miss mirrors in several of these situations, faults add up quickly and can turn a good drive into a fail. The RSA guidance is clear: the examiner needs to see that you are aware of traffic behind and beside you before every change of speed, position, or direction.

The good news is that mirror use is a habit, not a talent. Once it becomes automatic, you stop thinking about it and start actually driving on the information you see.

The three mirrors and what each one tells you

  • Centre mirror: gives you a general picture of traffic directly behind. Use it first in most routines.
  • Left door mirror: shows traffic on your left, cyclists, parked cars, and the kerb. Vital before turning left, moving off from a kerb, or pulling in.
  • Right door mirror: shows traffic on your right and behind. Vital before moving off, changing lane right, overtaking, or turning right.

When to check your mirrors on test

A useful rule: mirrors come before every decision, never after. The classic routine is Mirror, Signal, Manoeuvre (MSM), often expanded to Mirrors, Signal, Position, Speed, Look (MSPSL). Here are the moments the examiner is watching most closely.

Moving off

Centre mirror, right mirror, blind spot over your right shoulder, then signal if needed and pull out. This is expected every single time you move off from a stopped position, including after the reverse manoeuvre and after any pull-in.

Before signalling

Always check the mirror before you indicate. Signalling first and then checking is a common fault. The mirror check tells you whether it is safe to signal your intention in the first place.

Before changing speed

Slowing for a junction, roundabout, pedestrian crossing, or a speed limit drop? A centre mirror check tells you whether the driver behind has noticed. This is especially important on approach to national speed limit sections around rural centres like Carlow, Naas, or Mullingar, where speeds change quickly.

Before changing direction or lane

Turning right at a junction, changing lane on a dual carriageway, or exiting a roundabout all need a mirror check into the relevant door mirror before the movement. In busy Dublin centres like Finglas, Raheny, or Tallaght, examiners see mirror faults constantly at multi-lane roundabouts.

On roundabouts

Check the left door mirror before every exit. This is one of the most missed checks in the country. Cyclists and motorbikes filter along the left, and the examiner needs to see you clear that space before you leave the roundabout.

Before stopping or pulling in

Centre mirror first, then left door mirror before you drift toward the kerb. Pulling in without checking is a guaranteed fault.

What examiners actually see

Examiners are trained to spot genuine eye movement, not head theatre. A small, deliberate glance is enough. What loses marks is:

  • Signalling before looking
  • Only using the centre mirror and ignoring the door mirrors
  • Checking mirrors but not acting on what you see (for example, braking hard when a car was already close behind)
  • Forgetting the left mirror on roundabout exits
  • Skipping the blind spot check when moving off

Practicing mirror routines before test day

Mirror habits stick when you build them into every drive, not just lessons. Try this on your next practice run:

  • Say out loud what you see: "car close behind", "clear left", "cyclist on inside". This forces you to process, not just glance.
  • Before every signal, tap your finger on the steering wheel as you check the mirror. It links the two actions.
  • Drive a full practice route around your test centre and count how many roundabout exits you take without checking the left mirror. The number is usually higher than learners expect.

If you are practicing on real test-area roads, DriveFlow route guides for centres like Wicklow, Navan, Waterford, and Killarney show you the junctions and roundabouts examiners actually use. You can see every published centre on the routes page.

Final tips

Mirror checks are not about impressing the examiner. They are about proving that your decisions are based on real information. Keep the movements small, keep them frequent, and always check before you act. Do that consistently, and one of the most common test-day faults quietly disappears from your report sheet.
