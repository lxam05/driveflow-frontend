Killester Driving Test Routes: What to Expect

Updated August 2026

Killester is one of the busier north Dublin test centres, tucked into a residential pocket off the Howth Road. If you have a test booked here, you already know the traffic can be unpredictable and the corridors are tighter than what many learners practice in the suburbs further out. This guide walks you through the roads examiners commonly use, the pressure points that trip learners up, and how to prepare so you feel in control on the day.

Where the Killester test centre sits

The centre operates from a compact base off the Howth Road, close to Killester DART station and the junction network that feeds into Clontarf, Raheny, Artane and Killester Avenue. Because it is so close to the coast road and the Malahide Road, you can end up on a mix of quiet estates, busy dual-carriageway feeder roads, and narrow shopping streets all within a single test.

Typical corridors on a Killester test route

The RSA does not publish a fixed route, and examiners draw from a wide local road network with variation from test to test. That said, the roads reported most often by learners in this area include:

  • Howth Road , heavy traffic, bus lanes, and multiple side-road pull-outs. Watch your lane discipline and mirror checks.
  • Clontarf Road and the coast route , a 60 km/h stretch where consistent speed matters. Underdriving here is a common fault.
  • Killester Avenue and Middle Third , narrower residential roads with parked cars on both sides, requiring careful positioning.
  • Malahide Road , faster-moving traffic and multi-lane junctions, often used to test lane changes and roundabout approach.
  • Collins Avenue East , long straight with schools, side roads, and pedestrian crossings; examiners often watch observation here.
  • Estates around Brookwood and St Anne's , quiet residential streets typically used for the reverse around a corner or turnabout.

Common pressure points

The Killester DART bridge and level crossing area

The road layout around the DART line can catch learners out. Approaching drivers sometimes hesitate at the wrong moment, or drift out of position because of the narrow lane width. Practice driving through this section from multiple directions so it feels familiar.

Roundabouts near the Malahide Road

Multi-lane roundabouts are a well-known fail point across north Dublin. Signal on entry and exit, choose the correct lane early, and do not straddle. If you are unsure about Dublin roundabouts more generally, our Killester routes page highlights specific examples used near the centre.

Bus lanes and cycle lanes on Howth Road

Killester learners often get marked for drifting into a bus lane during operational hours, or for not checking blind spots before crossing a cycle lane. Know the bus lane times on your route and make your observation obvious.

Speed management

Killester routes shift between 30, 50 and 60 km/h zones quickly. Driving too slowly on Clontarf Road or the Malahide Road, or missing a return to 50 km/h once you leave the coast section, is a very common fault. Read every speed sign and adjust promptly.

Manoeuvres you should expect

Examiners at Killester typically ask for one manoeuvre, drawn from:

  • Reverse around a corner (often into a quiet estate off Middle Third or near Brookwood)
  • Turnabout (three-point turn) on a wider residential street
  • Hill start on one of the gentle inclines in the area

Parallel parking is not currently part of the category B test, but strong general control and clutch skill are still assessed throughout the drive.

How to prepare for a Killester test

  1. Drive the area at test-day times. Traffic on the Howth Road at 10am is very different from a Saturday morning. Match your practice slot to your test slot.
  2. Practice the tight residential streets. Get comfortable with narrow roads and parked cars on both sides.
  3. Rehearse roundabout entries on the Malahide Road and near Artane. Lane choice is the single biggest issue.
  4. Know the speed-limit changes along Clontarf Road and coming back inland.
  5. Review your pre-drive checks and secondary controls the examiner will ask about at the start.
