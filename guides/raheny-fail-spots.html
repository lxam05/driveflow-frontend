Top 10 Fail Spots on the Raheny Driving Test

Updated May 2026 · A DriveFlow guide for learners testing at Raheny

Raheny is one of the busier Dublin test centres, and the area gives examiners plenty of variety — narrow estate roads, the Howth Road, the Malahide Road, the Kilbarrack Road, the coast road past St Anne's Park, and a couple of awkward junctions that catch learners out every week. Examiners draw from a local road network, so no two routes are identical, but certain spots come up again and again in learner debriefs.

Here are the ten places learners most commonly report losing marks on the Raheny test — and exactly what to do at each one.

  • 1. The Howth Road / Watermill Road junction

    This signalised junction is right on the test centre's doorstep. Learners often roll forward in the right-turn lane while the filter is red, or hesitate when the light goes green and an oncoming bus blocks the view. How to avoid it: stop behind the white line, hold the wheel straight, and only edge out when you can see clear road past the central island.

  • 2. Mini-roundabout at Kilbarrack Road / Tonlegee Road

    The painted dome catches people out. Learners drive straight over it as if it isn't there, or fail to give way to traffic already on the roundabout from the right. How to avoid it: treat it like any roundabout — give way to the right, signal left on exit, and steer around the dome, not over it.

  • 3. Lane discipline on the Malahide Road dual carriageway

    Hanging in the right lane after overtaking is a classic Raheny fault. Examiners want to see you move back to the left lane once it's safe and clear. How to avoid it: mirror, signal, and tuck back in as soon as you've passed — don't sit in lane 2.

  • 4. The Watermill Road / Bettyglen estate entrance

    Tight turn-in, parked cars on both sides, and a school nearby. Learners commonly clip the wrong side of the road on the turn or fail to check for kids stepping out. How to avoid it: slow right down before the turn, keep to your own side, and scan the footpath as you enter.

  • 5. Roundabout at the Coast Road / James Larkin Road

    Approaching from St Anne's Park, learners often pick the wrong lane for going straight ahead, then drift across at the exit. How to avoid it: read the road markings early, stay in the left lane for the first or second exit, and don't change lanes on the roundabout itself.

  • 6. Pedestrian crossings on the Howth Road near the village

    Two zebras and a signal-controlled crossing in close succession. Learners stop too late, or creep forward while someone is still on the crossing. How to avoid it: cover the brake as you approach, and wait until the pedestrian is fully on the footpath before moving off.

  • 7. Reversing around the corner in a Raheny estate

    The estates off Springdale Road and around Bayside are favourites for the reverse manoeuvre. Mounting the kerb, swinging too wide, or not checking blind spots is the usual cause of failure. How to avoid it: practice on quiet residential corners with a similar camber. Keep it slow, look over your shoulder, and use all your mirrors.

  • 8. Yield at Saint Assam's Avenue onto the Howth Road

    Visibility is restricted by parked cars, and learners often emerge without a proper second look right. How to avoid it: creep slowly, stop if you can't see, and only commit when you're sure of a clear gap — don't rely on the first glance.

  • 9. Box junction near Kilbarrack DART station

    Learners enter the yellow box when their exit isn't clear, which is an automatic fault. How to avoid it: wait behind the box until you can see your way through. If the car in front stops in the box, that's their problem — don't follow them in.

  • 10. Hill start on side roads off Watermill Road

    Several of the residential roads have a noticeable gradient, and examiners will sometimes ask for a hill start here. Rolling back, stalling, or pulling out without checking the right blind spot is what fails people. How to avoid it: handbrake on, find the bite, mirror and blind-spot check, signal, and release smoothly.

Practicing the right roads matters

The Raheny test isn't designed to trick you — it's designed to see whether you can drive safely on the roads the centre actually uses. The single best thing you can do is spend your last few lessons driving the local network: the Howth Road, the Malahide Road, the estates off Watermill Road, and the coast road around St Anne's. Knowing the lane layouts and the awkward junctions in advance means you're making driving decisions, not navigation decisions, on the day.
