Naas Driving Test Routes: Roads, Junctions and Fail Spots

Updated 19 August 2026 · DriveFlow local guide

The Naas test centre sits just off the town in Monread, and the examiner will take you through a mix of housing estates, tight urban junctions, national roads, and Naas's famously busy roundabouts. If you have been practicing in quieter parts of Kildare, the pace here can catch you out. This guide walks you through the roads and junctions you are most likely to meet, plus the fail spots learners consistently report around Naas.

Where the Naas test centre is and what to expect

The RSA test centre in Naas is located in the Monread area, close to the town centre and only minutes from the Naas Road (R445) and the M7. Because of that location, examiners have a huge variety of roads to draw from, and no two tests are identical. Expect the examiner to combine slower estate driving in Monread or Sallins Road, a stretch of higher-speed dual carriageway or national road, and at least two or three roundabouts.

Full test time on the road is usually around 30 to 40 minutes, and one reverse manoeuvre (turnabout, reverse around a corner, or reverse parking) is standard. For the official rules and grade sheet, see the RSA.

Typical Naas route corridors

Examiners draw from a local road network rather than one fixed loop, but these are the areas learners most commonly report driving through on a Naas test.

Monread and Sallins Road

Expect estate driving right out of the centre. Watch your speed (30 km/h zones), parked cars on both sides, and pedestrians near schools. The Sallins Road heading north gets busy at peak times, and the junction near the Sallins Road roundabout is a common observation slip point.

Newbridge Road (R445) and Naas town

The R445 towards Newbridge gives you the higher-speed section examiners often use to test lane discipline and safe overtaking discipline. Coming back into Naas via the Dublin Road brings you to the town's one-way system, where lane choice and mirror-signal-manoeuvre routines are tested closely.

Ballymore Eustace Road and Blessington Road (R410)

These roads take you out into rural 80 km/h stretches with hidden dips, blind bends, and the occasional slow tractor. Examiners want to see confident but safe progress. Sitting at 50 km/h in an 80 zone with clear road ahead will lose you marks for undue hesitation.

The roundabouts

Naas is famous for its roundabouts, and you will almost certainly meet more than one. The Maudlins roundabout, the Newhall roundabout, the Sallins Road roundabout, and the multi-lane junctions near the M7 interchange all appear regularly on tests. Lane discipline and signalling on exit are the two things examiners mark hardest here.

Common Naas fail spots

  • Roundabout lane discipline. Taking the wrong lane on approach to Maudlins or Newhall, or drifting between lanes on the roundabout itself, is the single most reported fault in Naas.
  • Signalling off roundabouts. Forgetting to signal left on exit is an easy but very common mark.
  • Observation at the Sallins Road junction. Learners often creep out without a proper right-then-left check.
  • Speed on the Newbridge Road. Missing the change from 60 to 80 (or the drop back to 50) is a frequent grade 3 fault.
  • Estate driving in Monread. Parked cars on both sides mean you need to give way properly and read oncoming traffic. Hesitation and progress faults appear here.
  • The town one-way system. Wrong lane choice near the Dublin Road / Main Street junction leads to last-second lane changes, which examiners will fault.

How to prepare for a Naas test

Practice on the exact roads described above, at the same time of day as your test slot. Naas at 09:15 on a Tuesday is very different from Naas at 14:30. If you are only driving at weekends, book a lesson or two on weekday mornings so nothing surprises you.

Drive every roundabout in the Naas ring more than once. Repetition is the only real cure for lane-choice hesitation. Do at least one full mock test on a commonly reported Naas route with your sponsor or instructor, including a reverse manoeuvre in a residential estate off Sallins Road or Monread.
