Driving Test Routes Near Me: How to Find Your Local Test Routes

Updated 31 October 2026 · DriveFlow

If you're prepping for the RSA driving test, one of the smartest things you can do is practice on the exact roads examiners actually use. The RSA doesn't publish a single fixed route for any test centre, but examiners do pull from a well-known local road network around each one. Knowing those roads before test day takes a huge chunk of the stress out of the drive.

This guide walks you through what "test routes near me" really means, how to find the routes examiners use at your local centre, and how to practice them properly in the weeks before your test.

Why practicing local test routes matters

Examiners work in a defined radius around each test centre. In most Irish centres that's roughly a 15 to 20 minute loop, taking in a mix of housing estates, roundabouts, national road stretches, and tricky junctions that are known locally for catching learners out.

When you practice those exact roads, three things happen:

  • You stop being surprised by sudden speed-limit changes (50 to 80, or 60 to 100).
  • You learn the lane discipline needed at local roundabouts before you're under test pressure.
  • You already know where the fail-heavy spots are, so observation and signalling become automatic.

Generic lessons in a different town won't give you that. A learner who's done ten hours on their actual RSA test-centre roads is almost always calmer on the day than one who hasn't.

How to find driving test routes near you

There are a few ways learners typically go about this:

1. Ask instructors who teach locally

Local EDT instructors know the test-area roads inside out. If you're taking lessons near your test centre, ask your instructor to cover the main corridors examiners use, not just the easiest practice loops.

2. Observe at the test centre

Some learners sit outside their centre and watch which way cars turn when they leave the car park. That gives you a feel for the first two or three turns, but it won't show you the full loop.

How often should you practice test routes?

There's no magic number, but a solid pattern looks like this:

  • 3 to 4 weeks out: drive each main route once with a sponsor driver, slowly, to spot junctions, speed changes, and roundabouts.
  • 2 weeks out: repeat the busier routes at the same time of day your test is booked. Traffic patterns change by hour.
  • Final week: focus on your weak junctions. Loop the same tricky roundabout four or five times until lane choice and observation feel automatic.

Learners who do this consistently tend to walk into the test centre already familiar with 70% of what the examiner is going to throw at them.

Common mistakes when practicing routes

  • Only practicing at quiet times. If your test is at 11am on a Tuesday, driving the route at 8pm on a Sunday won't prepare you for the actual traffic.
  • Memorising instead of understanding. You won't get the exact same route on the day. Learn the roads, not a script.
  • Ignoring the housing estates. Nearly every Irish test ends with a reverse or turnabout in a quiet estate. Know a few near your centre.
