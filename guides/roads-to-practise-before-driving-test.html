What Roads Should You Practice Before Your Driving Test?

Published 1 November 2026 · DriveFlow

If you want to pass the RSA driving test first time, the single most useful thing you can do is practice on the roads around your test centre. Examiners draw from a local road network, not a secret route list, so the junctions, estates and roundabouts you drive on test day will almost always be within a few kilometres of where you set off. Here is what to focus on, and how to find the right roads near you.

1. The streets immediately outside the test centre

The first two minutes of your test are usually the most nerve-wracking. You will pull out of the test centre, be asked to make an early turn or two, and settle into the drive. If those first streets feel familiar, your nerves settle quickly and your observations improve.

Drive the roads directly outside the centre at least a handful of times, in both directions. Pay attention to speed limit changes, give-way markings, and anywhere the road narrows. Centres like Tallaght, Finglas and Raheny all sit on busy urban roads where early lane choice matters.

2. Housing estates and residential roads

Almost every Irish test includes a loop through a quiet estate. This is where the examiner tests your low-speed control, your observation at junctions with poor visibility, and your awareness of parked cars, children and reversing vehicles.

You should be comfortable with:

  • Unmarked crossroads where priority is not obvious
  • Cul-de-sacs and tight turnarounds
  • Narrow roads with cars parked on both sides
  • Speed ramps, chicanes and 30 km/h zones

Estates near centres like Naas, Navan and Mullingar are commonly used for the turnabout or reverse around a corner.

3. Roundabouts of every size

Roundabouts are one of the biggest fail points on the Irish test. You need practice on mini-roundabouts, standard single-lane roundabouts, and the larger multi-lane roundabouts you find on ring roads and national routes.

Focus on lane discipline, signalling on exit, and observation over your left shoulder as you leave. If you are testing in Dublin or any larger town, pick a route that strings three or four different roundabouts together and drive it until lane choice becomes automatic. Centres like Dun Laoghaire, Waterford and Athlone all feature a good mix.

4. National roads and higher speed limits

Most tests include a stretch on a national road or dual carriageway where the speed limit climbs to 80 or 100 km/h. Examiners want to see that you can hold your speed, keep a safe following distance, and make confident lane changes with proper mirror checks.

If you only ever practice in town, this section will catch you off guard. Rural centres like Ballina, Carlow and Portlaoise rely heavily on national roads, so put proper time into them.

5. Hills, junctions on gradients and tricky turns

A hill start on a busy junction is a classic test scenario. Look for junctions in your area where you have to stop on a slope, give way, and move off smoothly without rolling back. Right turns across oncoming traffic on a narrow road are another weak spot for many learners.

How to find your local test routes

You will not get an official list of routes from the RSA, and examiners vary their routes to stop learners memorising them. The best approach is to drive the whole local area so nothing feels unfamiliar.

DriveFlow makes this easier. For each test centre, we publish the roads, estates and junctions examiners commonly use, with Google Maps links you can open on your phone and follow. Pick your centre on the test centre routes page and plan your practice sessions around the real area, not generic advice.

How often should you practice?

Aim for two or three focused sessions per week in the month before your test, each 45 to 60 minutes long. Mix the environments: one estate and manoeuvres session, one roundabouts and town session, one national road and higher speed session. In the final week, drive your actual test centre area at the same time of day your test is booked for, so you see the traffic you will see on the day.
