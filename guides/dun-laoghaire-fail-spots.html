Top 10 Fail Spots in the Dun Laoghaire Driving Test

Published 31 May 2026 · DriveFlow

The Dun Laoghaire test centre sits right in the heart of one of Dublin's busiest coastal suburbs, and that traffic pattern shapes every test that leaves the building. Tight residential streets, multi-lane roundabouts, steep hills, and constant bus and DART crossings give examiners plenty to assess in 30 minutes. Below are 10 of the most commonly reported fail spots from learners who have sat the test here, along with the kind of mistake that catches people out at each one.

1. Pottery Road roundabout

This roundabout sees heavy commuter traffic from the N11 side and learners often hesitate at the give-way line, then accelerate into a gap that was never really there. Examiners want a confident decision: either commit smoothly or stop fully. Practice reading the roundabout as you approach, not at the line itself.

2. Lane discipline on York Road

York Road has a few changes in road markings and parked cars that push you out toward the centre line. Learners commonly fail here for poor lane positioning or crossing a continuous white line to overtake a parked vehicle without checking mirrors first. Treat every parked car as a manoeuvre: mirror, signal, position.

3. The Monkstown Avenue / Carrickbrennan Road junction

This staggered junction trips learners up because visibility to the right can be blocked by parked cars. Edging out without a second proper look is a classic dangerous fault. Always come to a complete stop, then creep forward with another check before committing.

4. Mounttown Road hill start

Hill starts around Mounttown are a frequent setup for the test. Rolling back, stalling, or releasing the handbrake too early all show up here. If you drive an older clutch, find a quiet hill nearby and practice clean, smooth take-offs until they are second nature.

5. The Stradbrook Road roundabout

Smaller mini roundabouts like this one catch learners who do not signal on exit, or who hug the centre line when they should be in the left lane. Mini roundabouts still need full observation and signalling discipline, even when they look almost insignificant.

6. Newtownsmith and the seafront stretch

The road along the coast feels open and tempting to speed up on, but the limit drops and there are pedestrian crossings near the pier. Learners often pick up speeding faults here without realising it. Keep glancing at the speedo, especially after a 50 km/h sign.

7. George's Street Lower

Buses pulling out, taxis stopping, and pedestrians stepping off the kerb make this stretch one of the trickiest parts of the route. The fail usually comes from following too closely behind a bus or not anticipating a door opening. Hold back, leave a clear gap, and scan constantly.

8. Right turn at Tivoli Road

Turning right across oncoming traffic onto or off Tivoli Road catches learners who do not position correctly in the lane or who creep into the junction without a proper observation cycle. Set up your position early, take the gap when it is genuinely safe, and do not drift wide on exit.

9. Estate driving around Glenageary

Examiners regularly route through quiet estates near Glenageary to test slow-speed control, observation at unmarked crossroads, and reversing manoeuvres. The fault here is usually treating estates as low risk and dropping observation. Treat every unmarked junction as a yield and keep your eyes moving.

10. Returning to the test centre on Crofton Road

Plenty of learners relax on the way back and pick up a last-minute fault: a missed mirror check, a sloppy signal, or stopping awkwardly at the centre. The test is not over until the handbrake is on and the engine is off. Stay sharp right to the kerb.

How to prepare for the Dun Laoghaire route

None of these spots are impossible, but they reward learners who have driven the area in advance. Examiners draw from a local road network with variation, so the goal is broad familiarity, not memorising one route. Spend lessons covering the seafront, the estates around Glenageary and Monkstown, and the busier roundabouts at different times of day.
