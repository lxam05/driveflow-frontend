Cork Driving Test Routes Explained

Published 28 July 2026 · DriveFlow

Cork has more driving test centres than any other county in Ireland, and each one uses a very different road network. A learner sitting the test in Wilton will drive through busy city junctions and complex roundabouts, while someone testing in Skibbereen will spend most of the test on rural regional roads with sudden speed limit changes. If you are preparing for a test anywhere in Cork, knowing which centre you are booked into (and what the local roads throw at you) is the single most useful thing you can do before test day.

This guide walks through each of the main Cork test centres, what the routes are like, and where to find dedicated route practice for your centre.

Why local route practice matters in Cork

The RSA does not publish official test routes, and examiners draw from a local road network with variation. That means no two tests are identical, but the pool of roads, junctions, and roundabouts an examiner picks from is limited to the area around each centre. In Cork, that pool changes dramatically depending on where you book.

Wilton examiners are drawing from South Douglas Road, the Bishopstown roundabouts, and Model Farm Road. Mallow examiners pull from the N72 approaches, town-centre one-way systems, and the surrounding rural R-roads. Practicing in your test centre's specific area (not just "around Cork") is what turns a nervous learner into someone who has already seen every junction the examiner is likely to use.

The main Cork test centres

Wilton (Cork City)

Wilton is the busiest test centre in Cork and one of the busiest in Munster. Routes typically cover Bishopstown, Model Farm Road, Wilton Roundabout, and the residential estates off Sarsfield Road. Expect heavy traffic, multi-lane roundabouts, and plenty of lane discipline questions. Common fail points include the Wilton and Bishopstown roundabouts, and observation at the busier T-junctions off the Model Farm Road.

Ballincollig

Ballincollig serves the west side of Cork city and the surrounding commuter towns. Routes usually include the Main Street, the Poulavone roundabout, and residential estates around Muskerry and Innishmore. You will likely be tested on the transition between the N22 dual carriageway sliproads and the slower town roads, so speed adjustment and merging are key.

Mallow

Mallow is a mid-sized centre covering North Cork. Routes cover the town centre one-way system, the N72 approaches, and rural roads out towards Buttevant and Doneraile. Rural National Speed Limit signs, tight country bends, and reversing around corners in narrow estates are all common. Junction observation on the R-roads catches a lot of learners out.

Skibbereen

Skibbereen serves West Cork. Test routes lean heavily on regional and rural roads, with fewer roundabouts than the city centres but more challenging rural driving. Expect the N71, narrow country lanes, and hill starts on undulating terrain. Meeting oncoming traffic on narrow rural roads is a frequent test element here.

Mitchelstown

Mitchelstown is a smaller North Cork centre. Routes cover the town square, the R665 out towards Ballyporeen, and residential estates near the Convent Hill area. The town's one-way streets and priority-to-the-right junctions on quieter roads catch out learners who have only practiced in cities.

Which Cork centre is easiest?

There is no official ranking of Cork centres by difficulty, and the RSA is clear that examiners apply the same standards everywhere. That said, learners often report that rural centres feel less pressured because of lighter traffic, while city centres like Wilton demand more roundabout confidence and observation. Booking a rural centre is not a shortcut though. Rural routes bring their own challenges: narrow roads, tractors, poor sight lines, and sudden 80 km/h limits on winding roads.

The right centre for you is the one whose roads you have practiced most. If you have spent 40 hours driving around Bishopstown, book Wilton. If you learned to drive out around Doneraile, book Mallow.

Preparing for your test day

Whichever centre you sit in, the fundamentals stay the same: your EDT lessons, your pre-test practice, and knowing the local road network.
