Mulhuddart Driving Test Routes: What to Expect

Updated 16 September 2026 · DriveFlow

The Mulhuddart test centre sits inside the Blanchardstown corporate park, and it feeds one of the busier learner-driver catchments in west Dublin. If you are booked here, you are going to spend most of your test on a mix of dual carriageways, tight residential estates, and multi-lane roundabouts. This guide walks you through the roads examiners commonly use, the pressure points that catch learners out, and how to structure your practice before the big day.

Where the test centre is

The centre is located in Blanchardstown Corporate Park 2, off the N3 corridor. Examiners typically leave the corporate park quickly and head into one of a few broad areas: the residential estates around Tyrrelstown and Hollystown, the older village roads through Mulhuddart itself, and the busier commercial network around Blanchardstown Shopping Centre. You will not know your route in advance, and there is no single "Mulhuddart route" the RSA publishes, but there is a clear local road network examiners draw from with variation.

Typical route corridors

From the centre you can expect the examiner to point you towards one of the following corridors during a standard 30 to 40 minute test:

1. Tyrrelstown and Hollystown estates

Expect a run out along Church Road and into the estate networks around Tyrrelstown. These are quieter roads, but they are full of unmarked junctions, parked cars narrowing the carriageway, and speed ramps. Examiners often use this area for the reverse around a corner and to check your observation at minor junctions where the priority is not obvious.

2. Blanchardstown Village and Main Street

A common corridor takes you toward Blanchardstown Village via Church Road. The village has narrow one-way sections, pedestrian activity, buses pulling in and out, and awkward right turns onto Main Street. Lane discipline and smooth clutch control matter here.

3. N3 slip roads and the Snugborough interchange

Many learners will be brought onto the N3 briefly, or asked to negotiate slip roads and the multi-lane roundabouts at Snugborough and Coolmine. This is where lane choice, mirror-signal-manoeuvre routine, and confident merging get tested most directly.

4. Damastown and Porterstown Road

Damastown Industrial Park gets used for higher-speed national road driving and the roundabouts along the R121. Porterstown Road can appear too, with its long straight sections and the sharp turn near Coolmine train station.

Pressure points where learners lose marks

  • Roundabout lane choice: The Snugborough and Coolmine roundabouts have multiple lanes with painted arrows. Sitting in the wrong lane for a third exit is one of the most common grade 2 faults recorded around Mulhuddart.
  • Observation at estate junctions: In Tyrrelstown and Hollystown, learners often roll through T-junctions without proper right-left-right checks because visibility feels good. Examiners mark this hard.
  • Speed changes near schools: The area around Ladyswell and Powerstown has 30 km/h zones that appear quickly after 50 km/h stretches. Missing the change costs you.
  • Meeting oncoming traffic: Older sections of Church Road and Damastown Way narrow with parked cars. Hesitation, or failing to give way when you should, is a frequent fault.
  • The reverse around a corner: Often set on quieter estate corners with kerb-height changes. Practice this specifically on the kind of tight residential corner Mulhuddart uses, not just wide open ones.

How to prepare for a Mulhuddart test

The single best thing you can do is drive the actual road network the examiner will use, in the conditions you will be tested in. Book lessons or supervised practice at the same time of day as your test slot so you get used to the traffic patterns around the corporate park and the N3.

  1. Spend at least two sessions inside Tyrrelstown, Hollystown, and the estates behind the shopping centre practicing junctions, reversing, and turnabouts.
  2. Drive the Snugborough and Coolmine roundabouts repeatedly until lane selection is automatic.
  3. Practice the transition from N3 slip roads back into 50 km/h zones.
  4. Rehearse your technical checks (tyres, lights, fluids) in the car park before you go in. Examiners at Mulhuddart do ask them.
  5. Review the RSA's official test guidance at rsa.ie so nothing on the day surprises you.

What to expect on the day

Arrive at least 15 minutes early. There is parking outside the centre, but it fills up quickly. Bring your learner permit and glasses if you need them. The examiner will run through the paperwork, ask you a handful of the "rules of the road" questions, then have you demonstrate the secondary controls before you head out. The drive itself is around 30 to 40 minutes and usually includes one manoeuvre, one hill start where possible, and a stretch of higher-speed road.

Nothing on your Mulhuddart test should be a surprise if you have practiced the local network deliberately. Cover the corridors above, get roundabout lane discipline solid, and treat every estate junction like it might be watched. That is how you pass here.
