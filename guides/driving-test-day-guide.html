What Happens on the Day of Your Irish Driving Test

Published 22 June 2026 · DriveFlow

Test day in Ireland follows a fairly predictable pattern, and knowing exactly what is coming makes a big difference to how you drive. This guide walks you through the morning, the paperwork, the eyesight check, the technical questions, the drive itself, and the result. Whether your test is in Raheny, Naas, Tallaght, or a quieter centre like Carlow, the structure is the same.

Before You Leave the House

Get there with time to spare. Examiners do not wait. Aim to arrive at least 20 minutes before your slot. Use that time to check your car, calm your nerves, and re-read your secret questions.

Bring the following with you:

  • Your learner permit (the physical card, not a photo).
  • Your booking confirmation from the RSA.
  • Glasses or contact lenses if your permit specifies them.
  • A vehicle that is taxed, insured, NCT-valid, and displays L-plates on the front and back.

If anything is missing or the car is not roadworthy, the test will be cancelled and you will lose the fee.

Arriving at the Test Centre

Park outside the test centre, not in the staff bays. Most centres have a small waiting room with seats and a counter. Tell the receptionist your name and time. Some smaller centres, like Birr or Carlow Talbot Hotel, operate out of a hotel room rather than a dedicated building, so look for the RSA signage when you arrive.

Sit down, breathe, and wait. The examiner will call your name at the start of your slot.

Paperwork and Declaration

The examiner takes your learner permit, checks your photo, and asks you to sign a short declaration confirming the car is insured and roadworthy. They will also ask if you have any medical condition that affects your driving. Answer honestly. This part takes about two minutes.

The Eyesight Check

Outside the centre, the examiner will point to a parked car or a sign and ask you to read the registration plate from a set distance. If you wear glasses or contacts, wear them. If you struggle, the test ends here, so do not guess.

Technical Questions (the "Secret Questions")

Before you sit into the car, the examiner asks four questions from a list of around twenty. These cover things like:

  • What does this dashboard warning light mean?
  • How would you check your tyre pressure?
  • How would you know your brakes are working before moving off?
  • What documents must you carry when driving?

Then you sit into the car and demonstrate two or three "show me" controls: rear demister, hazard lights, headlights, wipers, fog lights. Practice these in your own car the night before. Fumbling a wiper switch is a common, easily avoided fault.

The Drive

The drive lasts roughly 30 to 40 minutes. The examiner sits in the front passenger seat with a clipboard and gives you directions in plain English: "take the next left", "follow the road ahead", "second exit at the roundabout".

You will be asked to do most of the following:

  • Move off from the kerb on a flat road and on a hill.
  • Drive on national roads, urban streets, and through housing estates.
  • Handle several roundabouts, junctions, and pedestrian crossings.
  • Perform a reverse around a corner.
  • Perform a turnabout (three-point turn) on a quiet road.
  • Pull in and move off safely on the side of a busier road.

Examiners draw from a local road network rather than one fixed route, so the exact roads vary. City centres like Finglas or Dun Laoghaire will throw heavy traffic and complex junctions at you, while rural centres like Clifden or Skibbereen lean more on narrow roads and national speed-limit stretches.

How Faults Are Marked

The examiner marks three categories on the report sheet:

  • Grade 1: a minor fault, noted but not counted against you.
  • Grade 2: a fault that affects safe driving. You fail if you get more than 8 of these in total, or 4 of the same type under one heading.
  • Grade 3: a serious or dangerous fault. One of these is an automatic fail.

Most fails happen on observation (mirrors and blind spots), progress (driving too slowly or hesitating at junctions), and roundabouts. If you want a deeper look, read why learners fail the driving test in Ireland.

Back at the Centre: Your Result

The examiner directs you back to the centre and asks you to park. They finish writing the report, then either hand it to you in the car or invite you back inside. They will tell you the result there and then: passed, failed, or passed with a small number of marks.

If you pass, you get a Certificate of Competency, valid for two years, which you bring to NDLS to apply for your full licence. If you fail, the examiner will go through every fault on the sheet with you so you know exactly what to work on.

Final Tips

  • Eat something light before the test. Low blood sugar plus nerves is a bad combination.
  • Do a 30 minute warm-up drive in the area before your slot if possible.
  • Treat the examiner like a passenger, not a judge. Talk through nothing, just drive normally.
  • One mistake is not a fail. Keep going.

Practicing in the right area is the biggest lever you have.
