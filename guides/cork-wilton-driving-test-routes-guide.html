Cork Wilton Driving Test Routes: Complete Guide

Updated October 2026

Wilton is one of Cork's busiest RSA test centres, sitting on the southwest edge of the city near Wilton Shopping Centre, Cork University Hospital, and the Bishopstown suburbs. If you're testing here, you're going to meet a mix of fast arterial roads, tight residential estates, multi-lane roundabouts, and school-zone traffic, often inside the same 30-minute route. This guide walks you through what examiners commonly include, where learners tend to lose marks, and how to prepare specifically for the Wilton road network.

About the Wilton test centre

The Wilton centre is located close to the Sarsfield Road area and operates alongside Ballincollig as one of the main Cork city testing options. It serves a huge catchment, from Bishopstown and Glasheen through to Togher, Model Farm Road, and out toward Ballincollig and the N22 approach. Because Wilton sits on major commuter corridors feeding CUH and the shopping centre, traffic is almost always heavy, and examiners use that to assess how you handle real pressure.

Typical route corridors around Wilton

While the RSA does not publish exact routes, learners and instructors consistently report that Wilton tests draw from a few recognisable corridors. Expect your route to combine two or three of these:

  • Sarsfield Road and Wilton Road: dense traffic flow past the hospital and shopping centre, with frequent lane changes and pedestrian activity.
  • Bishopstown Road and Model Farm Road: wider urban roads with a 50 km/h limit, used to assess steady progress, mirror checks, and junction approach.
  • Curraheen Road: narrower, often used for residential driving and oncoming traffic situations near parked cars.
  • Dennehy's Cross area: a complex multi-lane junction where lane discipline and signalling matter.
  • N22 / Ballincollig bypass approach: higher-speed dual carriageway driving, merging, and overtaking assessment.
  • Residential estates off Wilton and Bishopstown: commonly used for the reverse around a corner and turnabout manoeuvres.

Pressure points where learners lose marks

Wilton has a few recurring fail spots. Knowing them in advance means you can practice them deliberately rather than meet them cold on test day.

The Wilton Roundabout

The large roundabout outside the shopping centre catches a lot of candidates out. Lane choice on approach is critical, and traffic pulling in and out of the shopping centre entrance creates late surprises. Decide your exit early, pick the correct lane, and keep signalling clean.

Dennehy's Cross

This junction has multiple lanes, filter arrows, and a steady stream of buses and cyclists. Learners often drift between lanes or hesitate at the lights. Practice reading the road markings well before you arrive and committing to your lane.

CUH and shopping centre pedestrians

Expect pedestrians stepping out between parked cars near Wilton Shopping Centre and the hospital entrances. Examiners want to see you scanning, covering the brake, and reducing speed proactively.

Residential estate manoeuvres

Reverses are commonly set in quieter estates off Bishopstown Road or Curraheen Road. The corners can be tighter than they look, and parked cars often reduce your working space. Practice full observation (not just over your shoulder) and controlled clutch work.

N22 merging

If your route touches the Ballincollig bypass, you need to match traffic speed on the slip road and merge smoothly. Hesitating at the merge or stopping on the slip is a common grade 3 fault.

How to prepare for a Wilton test

  • Drive the area at test time. Wilton at 10am is a very different animal to Wilton at 2pm. Match your practice to your test slot.
  • Rehearse the roundabouts specifically. Wilton Roundabout, the Bandon Road roundabout, and the roundabouts along the N22 approach all have their own quirks.
  • Nail your observations. Cork examiners are strict on mirror and blind spot checks, especially before signalling and before moving off.
  • Practice manoeuvres in realistic estates. Pick residential streets with light traffic and parked cars, not empty car parks.
  • Know the speed limit changes. 50 km/h in built-up areas, 30 km/h in some estates, and 100 km/h on the bypass. Missing a change is an easy fault to avoid.

On test day

Arrive 15 minutes early, have your learner permit, EDT logbook, and glasses (if you wear them) ready. The examiner will check the car's basics (tyres, lights, indicators) and ask a few technical questions before you set off. Keep your pre-drive checks calm and methodical, it sets the tone for the whole test.

During the drive, the examiner will give clear directions. If you miss one, ask them to repeat it. Taking a wrong turn is not a fail, but reacting badly to it can be. Stay smooth, keep scanning, and treat every junction like it matters.
