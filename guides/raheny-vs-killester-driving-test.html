Raheny vs Killester Driving Test: Routes and Difficulty Compared

Updated: 23 August 2026

If you live on the northside of Dublin, chances are you're choosing between Raheny and Killester for your driving test. They sit only a few kilometres apart, share overlapping road networks, and can feel almost interchangeable on paper. In practice, though, the two centres pull from different route patterns and reward slightly different skills. Here's how they compare and how to decide which one suits you.

Both are official RSA test centres and both draw from a broad local road network. Examiners at each centre vary their routes, so don't expect a single "Raheny route" or "Killester route" to memorise.

Raheny: what the test feels like

Raheny routes tend to swing between two personalities. One minute you're on quiet estate roads off Watermill or St Assam's, checking for parked cars and cyclists, and the next you're merging onto the James Larkin Road with fast-moving coastal traffic. Learners often struggle with:

  • Lane discipline on the multi-lane roundabouts near Bull Island and the Wooden Bridge approach.
  • Speed limit changes as you drop from national-road pace back into 50 km/h estate zones.
  • Observation at the tight, angled junctions around All Saints Road and Springdale Road.
  • Reverse around a corner in estates where the kerb curves sharply.

For a full breakdown of common fail spots and route corridors, see the Raheny driving test routes page.

Killester: what the test feels like

Killester feels more consistently urban. You'll spend most of the test in steady city traffic on roads like Malahide Road, Collins Avenue, and Griffith Avenue, with plenty of traffic-light junctions, bus lanes, and pedestrian crossings. The typical challenges here are:

  • Bus lane awareness on Malahide Road, especially at peak times.
  • Right turns across busy traffic at signal junctions.
  • Lane selection approaching complex junctions like Donnycarney and Killester Avenue.
  • Yellow box junctions where hesitation or creeping causes marks.

You can see local corridors and hotspots in more detail on the Killester driving test routes page.

Which centre is "harder"?

Honestly, neither is objectively harder. Pass rates for individual Dublin centres shift year to year, and the RSA doesn't publish a single ranking that stays fixed. What matters more is which skill set you're stronger at:

  • Pick Raheny if you're confident on faster roads and multi-lane roundabouts, and you've practiced estate manoeuvres around St Anne's and Watermill.
  • Pick Killester if you're comfortable in dense city traffic, reading bus lanes, and handling signal-controlled junctions.

If you're weak at roundabouts, Raheny will punish you more. If you rush through traffic lights or drift into bus lanes, Killester will catch you out faster.

Preparation strategy for both

Wherever you sit your test, the most important thing is lessons and practice on the actual roads your examiner uses. Generic Dublin practice isn't enough. A few things worth doing before either test:

  • Drive the streets within a 3 km radius of your chosen test centre at different times of day.
  • Practice both manoeuvres (reverse around a corner, turnabout) in real residential estates, not empty car parks.
  • Rehearse the approach to the centre itself, so the start of your test isn't the first time you're driving that road.
  • Read up on the common reasons learners fail in Ireland and audit yourself honestly.

Other nearby options

If Raheny and Killester both have long waits, learners sometimes look at Finglas, Mulhuddart, or Dun Laoghaire. Each has its own quirks, and travelling to an unfamiliar centre for a shorter wait can backfire if you don't practice the local roads first. The best test centre is usually the one closest to where you actually drive.
