Roundabout Mistakes That Fail Learner Drivers in Ireland

Updated 28 June 2026 · DriveFlow

Roundabouts are one of the most common reasons learners pick up grade 3 faults on the RSA driving test. They demand quick decisions, accurate observation, and confident lane discipline, all at the same time. Get one of those wrong and the examiner will mark it. Get two wrong on the same roundabout and you are usually looking at a fail.

Below are the roundabout mistakes that come up again and again on test routes around Ireland, from busy multi-lane junctions near Tallaght and Finglas to smaller rural roundabouts near Naas, Carlow and Mullingar. Each one is fixable with focused practice.

1. Wrong lane on approach

This is the biggest single fault. Learners drift into the left lane out of habit, even when they are going right or full circle. The rule is simple: left lane for first exit and usually straight ahead, right lane for any exit past 12 o'clock. Always check the road markings and overhead signs early. On bigger roundabouts like the N7 junctions near Naas or the ring road roundabouts around Waterford and Limerick, lane arrows can be painted hundreds of metres before the junction. Read them in time.

2. No signal, or the wrong signal

You need to signal your intention to enter and your intention to leave. Going left, indicate left on approach. Going right, indicate right on approach and switch to left as you pass the exit before yours. Going straight ahead, no signal on approach, then left as you pass the previous exit. Forgetting that left signal as you exit is a classic test fault, especially on two-lane roundabouts where examiners want to see you communicating with traffic behind.

3. Stopping when the roundabout is clear

Yield does not mean stop. If there is a safe gap, take it. Learners who stop at an empty roundabout get marked for hesitation, and they often roll forward into traffic they did not check for properly. Practice rolling up to the give-way line at a slow, steady speed so you can flow on when it is safe.

4. Pulling out in front of traffic

The opposite mistake, and just as serious. Misjudging the speed of a car approaching from your right will get a grade 3 or worse, because it forces the other driver to brake. Treat any car already on the roundabout as having priority, even if they look slow. If in doubt, wait.

5. Poor observation around the roundabout

Examiners want to see your eyes moving. Look right as you approach, scan ahead to your exit, and crucially check your left mirror and left blind spot before you leave. Cyclists and motorbikes love the inside line on Irish roundabouts. A quick mirror check before exiting shows the examiner you know they are there.

6. Drifting between lanes

On multi-lane roundabouts, stay in your lane all the way around. Cutting the corner from the right lane into the left exit is a serious fault and can cause a collision. Keep your steering smooth and follow the curve of the lane you started in. This comes up a lot on test routes around Dun Laoghaire, Raheny, and Wilton in Cork.

7. Wrong speed on entry

Too fast and you cannot react to traffic already there. Too slow and you stall the car behind you. Aim to arrive at the yield line at a speed that lets you either stop cleanly or roll on without changing gear awkwardly. Second gear is usually the right answer for most Irish roundabouts.

8. Forgetting mini-roundabouts exist

Mini-roundabouts (the painted white dome) follow the same rules as full ones. You still yield right, signal properly, and pass around the centre, not over it, where possible. Treating one like a give-way junction is a quick way to lose marks.

How to practice roundabouts properly

  • Drive the actual test area at the same time of day as your test slot.
  • Pick one roundabout and approach it from every direction, taking every exit.
  • Talk through what you are doing out loud: lane, mirror, signal, observation, gear.
  • Watch experienced drivers and notice how early they read the lane markings.
  • Read the RSA Rules of the Road section on roundabouts the night before your test.

If you can demonstrate clean lane choice, correct signalling, and confident observation on every roundabout you meet, you take one of the biggest fail risks off the table.
