Top 10 Fail Spots in the Killester Driving Test

Updated August 2026 · Killester test centre, Dublin 5

The Killester test centre sits on Middle Third, tucked between the Howth Road, Collins Avenue and the DART line. It looks quiet on paper, but the local network throws a lot at learners in a short space: tight residential estates, busy arterial roads, a mix of signalised junctions and yields, and a couple of roundabouts that catch people out every week. Below are the ten spots and mistakes that come up again and again from learners sitting the test at Killester.

1. Pulling out of Middle Third onto the Howth Road

The first minute of your test often decides your nerves for the next 40. Middle Third meets a busy stretch of the Howth Road with fast-moving traffic in both directions. Learners commonly fail here by creeping into the junction, hesitating, or pulling out into a gap that is not quite there. Practice looking properly right, then left, then right again, and only commit when you can join at the flow of traffic without forcing anyone to brake.

2. The Collins Avenue / Killester Avenue junction

This signalised junction gets busy, and the right turn onto Collins Avenue East is where positioning goes wrong. Learners drift into the wrong lane, cut the corner, or fail to move forward into the box on a green arrow. Sit centred in your lane, watch for the filter, and complete the turn into the correct lane on the far side.

3. Roundabout at Kilbarrack Road / Howth Road

Not a huge roundabout, but examiners use it to check lane discipline, observation and signalling. Common fails: no signal on exit, wrong lane on approach for a right turn, or looking only right and not scanning ahead as you enter. Signal left as you pass the exit before yours, and keep both hands ready in case a cyclist appears on your left.

4. Estates off Middle Third and Brookwood

Killester examiners love a residential loop through the Brookwood estates or the roads off Middle Third. Learners fail here for speed (doing 45 in a 30), poor observation at unmarked crossroads, and clipping kerbs on tight left turns. Treat every unmarked junction as equal priority and slow right down.

5. Parked cars on Demesne and Brookwood Avenue

These roads often have cars parked on both sides, leaving a single working lane. Learners fail for not using the "meet and greet" rule: whoever has the obstruction on their side gives way. Hesitating in the middle, or barging through when an oncoming car is closer, both cost you marks.

6. The Howth Road bus lane

Bus lane hours catch learners out. Driving in an active bus lane, or swerving out of it late when you realise, is a common serious fault. Check the sign as you join the Howth Road and plan your lane early, especially if a right turn is coming up.

7. Killester DART bridge and pedestrian crossings

Near Killester station the road narrows under the bridge and there are pedestrian crossings on either side. Learners commonly fail for approaching too fast, not covering the brake near the crossing, or failing to yield to pedestrians already waiting. Ease off well before the crossing and make your intention to stop obvious.

8. Right turn onto Kincora Road

A classic Killester right turn where you need a proper gap in both directions and correct road positioning. Learners fail for stopping too far back from the centre line, taking a short gap and forcing oncoming traffic to slow, or turning into the wrong side of the road on completion.

9. The reverse around a corner or turnabout on a quiet estate road

Killester examiners will usually ask for a manoeuvre on a residential road. The most common fails are poor observation (not looking around the whole car during a turnabout), mounting the kerb on the reverse, and ignoring approaching traffic or pedestrians. Keep it slow, use full lock in stages, and stop if anyone needs to pass.

10. Returning to the test centre on Middle Third

Learners relax too early on the way back and pick up faults for speed, mirror checks, or a sloppy final park. The test is not over until the handbrake is on outside the centre. Keep your standard high right into the car park.

How to prepare for Killester specifically

General lessons will not be enough on their own. Spend structured practice hours on the Howth Road, Collins Avenue, Kilbarrack Road and the estates between them. Drive at different times of day so you see the traffic changes, especially around school run and DART commuter times. Combine that with the RSA's official advice on the driving test, which you can read on rsa.ie, and work through the fail points above with your sponsor or instructor.
