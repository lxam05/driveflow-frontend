Dun Laoghaire Driving Test: Roundabouts and Difficult Junctions

Published 13 September 2026 · DriveFlow

The Dun Laoghaire test centre off Cumberland Street sits in a dense coastal grid of Victorian terraces, one-way systems, and busy seafront roads. Examiners rarely follow one fixed route, but they consistently pull from the same local road network. That means the same handful of roundabouts and awkward junctions come up over and over again. If you know where they are and what marks people lose at each one, you take a lot of pressure off yourself on test day.

This guide walks through the pressure points around Dun Laoghaire, what to look for on approach, and how to avoid the common faults examiners flag.

1. The George's Street junctions

Upper and Lower George's Street form the retail spine of Dun Laoghaire, and the side road junctions here catch a lot of learners out. You will meet parked cars right up to the corners, cyclists filtering on the left, and pedestrians stepping out between buses.

The main faults here are late observation and hesitant emerging. When you pull out of a side road onto George's Street, you need a proper right-left-right check with your head, not just eyes. Examiners want to see you commit once the gap is there. Sitting frozen at a clear junction picks up a grade 2 fault for progress.

2. The seafront and East Pier area

The stretch along Queen's Road and Marine Road brings its own challenges. Lane discipline is the big one. As you approach the harbour area, lanes narrow, buses stop suddenly, and taxi ranks force you to plan an early move out. Signal early, mirror check, and hold your lane rather than drifting.

The mini roundabout near the People's Park is small but easy to misread. Treat it like any roundabout: give way to traffic from the right, signal your exit before you pass the previous exit, and keep your speed low and controlled.

3. The N11 and Rock Road roundabouts

Test routes often push out toward the N11 corridor and the Rock Road. This is where roundabout technique really gets tested. The multi-lane roundabouts at Blackrock and around the Stillorgan Road demand:

  • Correct lane on approach. Left lane for first exit or straight (where marked), right lane for third exit or U-turn. Read the road markings early.
  • Signal discipline. Signal right on approach if you are going past the 12 o'clock exit. Cancel and signal left as you pass the exit before yours.
  • Mirror check before every lane change on the roundabout itself. Motorbikes and cyclists filter here constantly.
  • Progress. If the gap is safe, take it. Rolling to a stop at a clear roundabout is a common fault.

4. Sallynoggin and Glasthule junctions

Routes heading inland toward Sallynoggin, Glasthule and Glenageary bring you into tight residential junctions with poor sightlines. Parked cars are the recurring hazard. You need to creep forward carefully, use every mirror, and only commit when you can actually see clear road, not when you assume it is clear.

The junctions around Eblana Avenue and Corrig Avenue are also worth practicing. They can feel like small side streets, but there is real traffic and pedestrians in both directions.

5. Uphill starts and hill junctions

Dun Laoghaire has more hills than people expect. Roads like Corrig Road, Tivoli Road, and the streets leading up from the seafront often include an uphill junction or hill start. Roll-back is a classic fault here. Use the handbrake, find the bite, and release smoothly. If you feel yourself rolling, you have not held the car properly.

Common marks lost at Dun Laoghaire junctions

  • Insufficient observation before emerging, especially on George's Street side roads.
  • Wrong lane on multi-lane roundabouts near the N11.
  • Late or missing signals on roundabout exits.
  • Hesitation at clear junctions leading to a progress fault.
  • Roll-back on hill starts around Tivoli Road and Corrig Road.
  • Not spotting cyclists in the door zone of parked cars.

How to practice these junctions

Book a few sessions during test hours (roughly 8:30am to 4pm on weekdays) so you experience the actual traffic patterns an examiner will see. Drive George's Street at lunchtime. Take the N11 roundabouts during rush hour. Do a full loop of the seafront when the DART crowds are moving. If a junction feels awkward, do it three times in a row until the lane choice and observation become automatic.
