Dun Laoghaire Driving Test Routes: Complete Local Guide

Updated 10 September 2026 · DriveFlow

If you're sitting your test at the Dun Laoghaire test centre, knowing the local road network in advance is one of the best things you can do. Examiners here draw from a set of typical corridors around the town centre, the coast road, and the residential estates behind the seafront. You won't get a fixed route, but the areas and junction types show up again and again. This guide breaks down what to expect and how to prepare.

About the Dun Laoghaire test centre

The Dun Laoghaire test centre sits close to the town and the seafront, which means your test will almost certainly involve a mix of busy urban streets, coastal roads with parked cars on both sides, and quieter residential estates for the manoeuvre. The area has a lot of pedestrian crossings, cyclists, and buses, so situational awareness is a big part of what examiners are watching.

Typical route corridors

Based on what learners commonly report, expect the examiner to send you through a combination of the following areas:

  • The town centre and George's Street: narrow lanes, one-way sections, taxis pulling in and out, and frequent pedestrian crossings.
  • The coast road (Marine Road, Queen's Road, and out toward Sandycove): parked cars on both sides, cyclists, and pinch points where you'll need to give priority correctly.
  • Monkstown and Deansgrange: steady residential roads with plenty of side turns, filter lanes, and traffic-light junctions.
  • Glenageary and the estates behind the DART line: quieter roads often used for the reverse around a corner or turnabout.
  • The N11 approach at Cornelscourt or Foxrock: if a dual carriageway section is included, this is where higher speed and lane discipline are assessed.

Pressure points to prepare for

Roundabouts

The Dun Laoghaire area has several roundabouts where learners lose marks, especially around Deansgrange, Cornelscourt, and the approach roads to the N11. Practice lane choice on approach, signalling on exit, and holding your position without drifting. If you struggle with roundabouts generally, the route practice gives you a chance to run them repeatedly.

Parked cars and priority

The coast road and the streets behind the seafront are lined with parked cars. Examiners want to see you assess who has priority, hold back when needed, and move through gaps without hesitation once it's your right of way. Hesitating too long or pushing through when it isn't your turn both cost marks.

Pedestrian and cyclist awareness

Dun Laoghaire is busy with foot traffic near the DART station, the People's Park, and the shopping streets. Cyclists use the coast road heavily. Mirror checks before every manoeuvre, and observation at zebra crossings, are non-negotiable.

Hill starts and slopes

The town rises away from the sea, so hill starts can appear naturally on your route. Practice moving off smoothly on an incline without rolling back, particularly at junctions on the slopes above the town.

The manoeuvre

Expect the reverse around a corner or the turnabout in one of the quieter residential estates, often in Glenageary or Sallynoggin. Choose an estate with a proper kerb line and practice controlling the car at low speed while checking your blind spots.

How to prepare in the weeks before your test

  1. Drive the commonly reported routes with a sponsor at the same time of day as your test slot. Traffic on the coast road looks very different at 10am versus 3pm.
  2. Do at least two full mock tests along a Dun Laoghaire loop, from pulling out of the centre to returning and parking.
  3. Spend one session focused only on roundabouts and one only on manoeuvres in a local estate.
  4. Review the RSA test rules on the RSA website so you're clear on what examiners mark against.
  5. On the morning of the test, drive the area for 20 to 30 minutes to settle your nerves and re-check the coast road traffic.

On the day

Arrive at least 15 minutes early. Bring your learner permit, and make sure your car is taxed, insured, and properly displaying L plates. The examiner will check the car before you start, so tyres, lights, and indicators all need to be in order. Once you're moving, treat it like any other drive: mirrors, signal, position, speed, look. The examiner isn't trying to catch you out, they're checking that you can drive safely in the environment around the Dun Laoghaire test centre.
