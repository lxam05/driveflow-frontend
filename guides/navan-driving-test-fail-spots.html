Top 10 Fail Spots in the Navan Driving Test

Updated 28 September 2026 · DriveFlow

Navan test centre sits on the Kells Road, so most tests start with a mix of busy urban traffic, tight estate roads around Johnstown and Windtown, and a run out onto the N51 or R147. Examiners draw from a local road network with plenty of variation, but the same trouble spots keep coming up in feedback from learners. Below are ten places and situations where candidates most commonly report failing in Navan, and how to handle each one.

1. The Kennedy Road roundabout

This roundabout is one of the busiest fail points for Navan tests. Learners often drift lanes exiting toward the town centre or fail to signal off in time. Approach in the correct lane early, check your left mirror before the exit, and signal left as you pass the exit before yours.

2. Merging onto the N51 (Athboy Road)

The slip onto the N51 heading west catches learners who either crawl on at 40 km/h into 80 km/h traffic or fail to check the blind spot before merging. Build speed on the slip, take a proper right shoulder check, and merge in a smooth line rather than a sharp swerve.

3. Johnstown estate roads

Examiners frequently take candidates through the residential streets around Johnstown for observation and speed control. Common fails here include missing pedestrians at unmarked junctions, not yielding to cars emerging from the right, and drifting over the 50 km/h limit. Treat every crossroads as a potential yield situation.

4. The R147 through Kilcarn

Speed adjustment is the big one on the R147. The limit changes as you pass into and out of built-up areas, and learners often stay too fast approaching town or too slow once the national road opens up. Watch the signs, not just the traffic ahead.

5. Windtown roundabout

Multi-lane roundabouts near Windtown trip up learners on lane discipline. Going straight ahead often means the left lane, but if there's a dedicated left-turn lane, you'll need the second lane. Read the road markings before you commit, and never change lanes inside the roundabout.

6. Reverse around the corner on a residential road

The reverse manoeuvre is usually done on a quiet side road off the Kells Road or in the Johnstown area. Fails come from mounting the kerb, swinging too wide, or forgetting to look out the back window and around for pedestrians. Go slowly, use full observation, and don't rush to finish.

7. Hill start on Trimgate Street or Ludlow Street

Navan's town centre streets have gentle inclines that catch learners out at traffic lights. Rolling back even a small amount is a marked fault. Practice smooth clutch control, or in an automatic, keep firm brake pressure until you're ready to move.

8. The Flowerhill junction

This busy junction near the town centre demands sharp observation and confident decision making. Hesitating at a clear gap is as bad as pulling out in front of traffic. Look, decide, go. Don't creep forward without a full stop if there's a stop line.

9. Pedestrian crossings on Brews Hill

There are several zebra and signalled crossings on the approaches to town. Learners commonly fail for not slowing on approach when pedestrians are clearly waiting, or for creeping over the stop line at signals. Cover the brake early and stop behind the line, not on it.

10. Turnabout on a narrow road

The three-point turn is often set on a tight residential road with parked cars and limited space. Fails come from clipping the kerb hard, not using mirrors on each direction change, or panicking and skipping observation. Take your time, check both ways before every move, and use full lock when you need it.

How to prepare for these spots

The best preparation is targeted practice on the actual roads examiners use. Drive the Kennedy Road roundabout at different times of day, run the N51 slip a few times, and get comfortable in the estates around Johnstown and Windtown. Book lessons with an RSA-approved instructor if any of the manoeuvres feel weak.
