Lane Positioning Mistakes on the Irish Driving Test

Published 19 July 2026 · DriveFlow

Lane positioning is one of the most common reasons Irish learners lose a test. It sounds simple, stay in your lane, but examiners are watching for something much more specific: whether you position the car correctly for the road ahead, and whether you commit to the right lane early enough. Drift, hesitation, or last-second lane changes will pick up a Grade 2 fault, and repeated errors turn into a Grade 3 fail.

Here are the lane positioning mistakes that come up on Irish driving tests, and how to fix them before you sit yours.

1. Sitting too close to the centre line on rural roads

On national roads and country lanes, learners often drift towards the white line because it feels safer than the verge. Examiners see it differently. You should be positioned roughly a third of the way in from the left, keeping a consistent gap from the edge without hugging the ditch. This is especially important at centres like Ballina, Clifden, and Carrick-on-Shannon where narrow rural roads dominate the route.

If an oncoming vehicle appears, ease left smoothly. Do not swerve or brake sharply.

2. Late lane selection at roundabouts

This is the single biggest fail point in urban test areas. If you are going past the 12 o'clock exit at a roundabout, you should already be in the right-hand lane on approach, indicating right, and staying right through the roundabout until the exit before yours. Learners drifting between lanes, or arriving in the wrong lane and cutting across, will fail.

Roundabouts feature heavily on tests from Tallaght, Finglas, Mulhuddart, Naas, and Wilton. Read the lane markings on the road, not just the signs, and commit early.

3. Straddling lanes on multi-lane roads

Dual carriageways and wide urban roads catch learners who never fully pick a lane. You should be central in your lane, with equal space on both sides. If you find yourself close to either line, correct gently with the steering wheel, not by drifting over several seconds. Straddling a lane will draw a fault every time.

4. Turning right from the wrong position

When turning right from a two-way road, your car should be positioned just left of the centre line, indicating in good time. Learners who stay in the middle of the lane block traffic behind them and pick up a fault for poor position. Learners who cross too far right into oncoming traffic pick up a much more serious fault.

At busy junction-heavy centres like Raheny, Killester, and Dun Laoghaire, right turns come up constantly and examiners are quick to mark them.

5. Wrong lane on approach to traffic lights

Filter lanes at traffic lights are painted on the road for a reason. If the left lane is marked left-turn only and you go straight from it, that is an automatic fail. Read the arrows on the tarmac at least two car lengths before the stop line. If you realise you are in the wrong lane, do not swerve across at the last moment. Follow the lane you are in and recover on the next street.

6. Cutting corners on left turns

A left turn should keep you tight to the left kerb without mounting or clipping it. Learners who swing wide into the oncoming lane before turning left are marked immediately. Practice keeping the wheel steady and turning at the correct moment, roughly when the kerb disappears from your left mirror.

7. Poor position in narrow estates

Housing estates are on almost every test route in Ireland. Parked cars on both sides force you to weave, and this is where position matters most. Stay left when you can, give parked cars a door's width of clearance, and slow down before you commit. Estates around Tallaght, Finglas, and Navan are known for tight residential sections.

How to fix lane positioning before your test

  • Practice on the actual roads your test centre uses. Route familiarity means you already know which lane you need.
  • Look further ahead. Most position faults come from looking 10 metres in front of the bonnet instead of 100 metres down the road.
  • Commit early at roundabouts and filter lanes. Late decisions are what examiners fault.
  • Ask your sponsor or instructor to sit in and call out your position every few minutes on a practice drive.
  • Review the RSA Rules of the Road section on lane discipline before test day.
