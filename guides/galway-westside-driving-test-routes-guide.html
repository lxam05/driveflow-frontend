Galway Westside Driving Test Routes: Complete Guide

Published 7 October 2026 by DriveFlow

If your test is booked at the Westside test centre in Galway, you're sitting one of the busier urban tests in the west of Ireland. The area mixes tight residential estates, fast dual carriageway sections, and a cluster of roundabouts that catch out learners who've only practiced on quieter roads. This guide walks you through the typical corridors examiners use, the pressure points you need to rehearse, and how to prepare so test day feels familiar rather than foreign.

About the Westside test centre

The Westside centre sits on the western edge of Galway city, near the Westside Shopping Centre and Seamus Quirke Road. It serves a huge catchment, from Salthill and Knocknacarra out to Rahoon, Newcastle, and the inner city. Because the area blends housing estates, retail traffic, school zones, and the N6/N59 corridors, your test can swing from 30 km/h estate roads to 80 km/h national road sections within minutes.

Westside is one of two Galway city centres. If you're deciding between locations, our Clybaun routes guide covers the other option, which has a slightly different road mix.

Typical route corridors

Examiners don't follow one fixed route, but they draw from a well-known local road network. The RSA (rsa.ie) rotates routes to cover a range of road types in the 30 to 40 minute test window. On a Westside test you can commonly expect to be sent along:

  • Seamus Quirke Road toward the Browne Roundabout, dealing with multiple lanes and bus lane markings.
  • Western Distributor Road through Knocknacarra, with its chain of roundabouts and 50 km/h limit changes.
  • Rahoon Road and Bishop O'Donnell Road, which test lane discipline and awareness of pedestrians near schools.
  • Residential estates around Shangort, Clybaun, and Rahoon for the reverse around a corner and turnabout manoeuvres.
  • Dr Mannix Road and the coast approach toward Salthill if time allows, which brings in parked cars and narrow two-way sections.

Pressure points to practice

The Browne Roundabout and Western Distributor roundabouts

These are the single biggest fail generator at Westside. The Browne Roundabout handles heavy traffic from the N6, Seamus Quirke Road, and the Quincentenary Bridge. You need confident lane selection on approach, correct signalling on exit, and the ability to read fast-moving traffic without hesitating in the middle of the junction. Practice each exit from each approach before test day.

Lane discipline on Seamus Quirke Road

This road has bus lanes, filter lanes, and busy junctions near the shopping centre. Drifting across lanes, straddling the bus lane during restricted hours, or last-minute lane changes will all be marked. Know when the bus lane is active and plan your position early.

Estate roads and the 30 km/h zones

Several estates around Rahoon and Knocknacarra are 30 km/h. Examiners will often take you in for the reverse around a corner or the turnabout. Watch your speed, your observation at junctions where visibility is limited by parked cars, and your use of mirrors before every manoeuvre.

School zones and pedestrian crossings

Westside covers several schools. If your test slot overlaps with drop-off or pick-up, expect heavy foot traffic. Cover the brake, scan both footpaths, and give way clearly at zebra crossings.

Manoeuvres you'll likely be asked

On most Westside tests you'll do two of the following in a quiet estate street:

  • Reverse around a corner (left-hand corner is most common)
  • Turnabout (three-point turn)
  • Hill start
  • Reverse parking (less common here but possible near the centre)

You'll also face the standard secondary controls check and roadside questions before you move off.

How to prepare for a Westside test

  1. Drive the actual roads. Book lessons or practice sessions that cover Seamus Quirke Road, the Western Distributor, and the estates behind Rahoon. Generic city driving isn't enough.
  2. Rehearse the roundabouts in both directions. Approach from Knocknacarra and from the city side so you know every exit.
  3. Pick a test slot off-peak if possible. Mid-morning or early afternoon avoids the worst of school and commuter traffic.
  4. Review the common fault categories. Observation at junctions, progress, and roundabout positioning are the most frequently marked grade 2 faults.
  5. Do a mock test with an instructor who knows Westside. Nothing replaces a run-through on the real road network.

After your test

Whether you pass or need a resit, the roads around Westside are worth knowing. The area connects into the N6, the Quincentenary Bridge, and the coast road to Salthill, which you'll likely drive regularly once you're on a full licence.
