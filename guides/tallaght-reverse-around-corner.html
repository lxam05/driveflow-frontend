Tallaght Reverse Around the Corner: Where to Practice and What Examiners Watch For

Updated September 2026 · DriveFlow guide for Tallaght learners

The reverse around the corner is one of the trickiest manoeuvres on the Irish driving test, and if you're testing at Tallaght test centre, it's almost guaranteed to appear. Examiners at Cookstown Industrial Estate use the estate's side streets and the quieter residential turns nearby, so knowing exactly where and how to practice can make the difference between a clean sheet and a Grade 3 fault.

This guide covers the best practice spots close to the test centre, what the examiner is really looking for, and the specific mistakes that catch Tallaght candidates out.

Where the manoeuvre usually happens on the Tallaght test

Tallaght examiners tend to pull you in to a quiet residential road for the reverse around the corner, often within a five to ten minute drive of Cookstown Industrial Estate. Common areas include:

  • Aylesbury and Kingswood estates, off the Belgard Road. Wide sweeping corners with light traffic, ideal for a controlled reverse.
  • Old Bawn side roads, near the Dodder river. The corners here are tighter and the camber can pull the car if you're not steady on the clutch.
  • Kilnamanagh estate, off the Greenhills Road. Junctions here often have parked cars on the approach, testing your observation.
  • Fettercairn and Jobstown residential roads. Slightly further out, but examiners do use these on longer routes.

Because the examiner picks a real corner on the day, you can't memorise one specific junction. What you can do is practice the same shape of corner: a left turn off a minor road into another minor road with room to line up beforehand.

What the examiner is actually watching for

The reverse around the corner is scored on three things: control, observation, and accuracy. In Tallaght, where estate roads can be narrow and cars are often parked close to junctions, observation is where most candidates lose marks.

Key point: The examiner is not timing you. Slow, steady, and safe beats fast every time. If you need to stop and let a car pass, that's a positive, not a fault.

Control

Clutch control is everything. The car should crawl backwards at walking pace or slower. If you're bouncing off the clutch or the engine is revving, you'll pick up a fault for vehicle control. Practice on a slight incline near Old Bawn to build confidence with the biting point.

Observation

You need to look all around, not just in the mirrors. The examiner wants to see your head turning:

  • Before you move off from the kerb.
  • Continuously through the turn, mainly out the rear window and over both shoulders.
  • Especially when the front of the car swings out into the road you're leaving.

In Tallaght estates, cyclists and kids on scooters appear from driveways with no warning, so genuine observation matters, not just performative head turning.

Accuracy

You should finish parallel to the kerb, roughly a half metre away, and straight. Mounting the kerb or ending up a full lane out from it will earn a fault.

The most common faults at Tallaght

  • Swinging too wide and drifting toward the far kerb. Usually caused by starting the steer too early.
  • Clipping the kerb on the inside. Caused by steering too sharply once the corner is halfway complete.
  • No observation to the right as the front of the car swings out. This is a Grade 2 fault waiting to happen on Kilnamanagh's narrower roads.
  • Stalling under pressure. If you stall, restart calmly, check mirrors and blind spots again, and continue.
  • Finishing too far from the kerb because you straightened up too early.

A simple method that works

  1. Pull up parallel to the kerb, about a car length before the corner. Handbrake on, neutral.
  2. Full observation: mirrors, both blind spots, out the rear window.
  3. Select reverse, find the biting point, release the handbrake.
  4. Reverse straight until the kerb starts to curve in your left wing mirror.
  5. Steer slowly to the left as the corner comes round. Keep checking behind and to the right.
  6. Straighten up when the car is parallel to the new kerb, continue back two or three car lengths, then stop.

Practice tips before test day

Do the manoeuvre on at least four different corners around the Tallaght area before your test. Vary the tightness, the camber, and the traffic level. Practice at different times of day so you're not thrown by a delivery van or a school run.
