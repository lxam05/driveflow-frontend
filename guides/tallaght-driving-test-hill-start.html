Tallaght Driving Test Hill Start Locations and Tips

Updated 7 September 2026 · DriveFlow

Hill starts trip up a lot of learners at Tallaght, and it is one of the few faults that is almost entirely preventable with local practice. The area around the test centre on the Cookstown Industrial Estate is genuinely hilly in places, with a mix of long gradients on the R113 and short, sharp inclines through the older residential estates near Old Bawn and Kilnamanagh. Examiners know exactly where to test you.

This guide covers the hill start spots most commonly used on the Tallaght route, what your examiner is actually watching, and how to stop yourself rolling back on the day.

Where examiners set up hill starts near Tallaght

Examiners rarely announce a hill start as "the manoeuvre." More often, you will end up performing one naturally when traffic lights turn red on an incline, or when you are pulled in on a sloped stretch of road. Below are the areas learners hit most often on Tallaght test routes.

Old Bawn Road and the climb toward Bohernabreena

Heading south from the Old Bawn roundabout, the road climbs steadily. Any stop here, whether behind a bus, at a pedestrian crossing, or when the examiner asks you to pull in on the left, becomes a hill start. The gradient is gentle but consistent, which is unforgiving on clutch control.

Kilnamanagh estate roads

The estate roads between Kilnamanagh Shopping Centre and Greenhills Road include several short but noticeable inclines. Junctions on these roads often require you to stop on a slope with limited visibility, then pull off cleanly into moving traffic.

The R113 (Belgard Road / Airton Road area)

Approaching the Cookstown roundabout from the north, you will often be asked to pull in on the left near industrial units. The camber and slight rise here catch people out, especially when there is a cyclist or parked car to clear on pull-off.

Firhouse Road approaches

If your route extends toward Firhouse, expect stops on inclines near the Ballycullen junction and around the school zones. Traffic lights that turn red as you approach an uphill line of cars are a classic hill-start scenario.

What the examiner is watching for

You do not need a textbook-perfect hill start. You need one that is safe and controlled. Specifically, the examiner is looking at:

  • Rollback. Any significant rollback (more than about half a car length, or into a vehicle behind you) is a serious fault. Even small rollbacks repeated across the test can add up to a grade 2.
  • Observation before moving. Mirrors and a right blind-spot check before you release the handbrake, every time.
  • Smooth pull-off. No stalling, no over-revving, no kangaroo hop. Clutch control matters more than speed.
  • Correct signalling. If you are pulling out from the kerb, signal right. If you are just moving off from lights, no signal is needed unless changing lanes.
  • Progress. Do not sit there for ten seconds finding the bite. Once the road is clear, go.

How to nail the hill start on test day

  1. Handbrake on, firmly. As soon as you stop on any incline, apply the handbrake. Do not rely on the clutch alone at lights or in queues.
  2. Find the bite before you release. Clutch to bite point, gentle gas (revs sitting steady around 1,500 rpm on a moderate hill). You should feel the bonnet lift slightly.
  3. Full observation. Right mirror, over-the-shoulder blind spot, signal if pulling out.
  4. Release the handbrake smoothly while holding the bite. If the car starts to roll, you did not have enough bite or gas.
  5. Ease off the clutch fully as you accelerate away. Do not ride it.

If you do stall, do not panic. Handbrake back on, restart, breathe, and go again. A single controlled stall is usually a grade 2 fault, not a fail. Panicking and rolling back into the car behind is far worse.

Where to practice before your test

Any of the roads listed above are fair game outside test hours. Aim for at least three sessions where you deliberately stop on hills, use the handbrake, and pull off cleanly. Old Bawn Road on a quiet Sunday morning is ideal, as is the loop through Kilnamanagh estate. Practice with a passenger who can tell you honestly whether you rolled back.

Also spend time in stop-start traffic on Belgard Road at rush hour. Real test conditions include impatient drivers behind you, and getting used to that pressure is half the battle.
