Finglas Driving Test Roundabouts Explained: Lane Positioning and Common Faults

Updated September 2026 · DriveFlow

Roundabouts are where a lot of Finglas tests are quietly won or lost. The test centre sits off Jamestown Road, and within a few minutes of pulling out you'll be dealing with the kind of busy multi-lane junctions that the RSA examiner uses to check your observation, lane discipline, and decision making. This guide walks through the roundabouts you're most likely to meet, how to sit in the lanes, and the faults that keep coming up on marking sheets.

The roundabouts that matter around Finglas

Examiners draw from the local road network rather than one fixed route, but Finglas tests almost always cover a mix of the following:

  • Mellowes Road / Ballygall Road roundabouts in the residential estates near the centre. Smaller, but tight lane markings and parked cars pushing you off line.
  • McKee Avenue and Finglas Road roundabouts feeding traffic between Finglas village and the N2 corridor.
  • The N2 / M50 interchange roundabouts at Junction 5. Big, multi-lane, and always busy. Expect heavy commercial traffic and lane-hoppers.
  • Charlestown and St Margaret's Road roundabouts north of the centre, where speeds are higher and signage arrives quickly.

Lane choice: get it right on approach

Most marks lost at Finglas roundabouts come from being in the wrong lane, not from bad car control. The rule of thumb the RSA expects you to apply:

  • First exit (left): approach in the left lane, indicate left, stay left through the junction.
  • Straight ahead (usually the second exit): approach in the left lane by default, no signal on approach, indicate left as you pass the exit before yours.
  • Any exit past 12 o'clock (right or U-turn): approach in the right lane, indicate right, then switch your signal to left after you've passed the exit before your one.

The catch at Finglas is that a lot of the bigger roundabouts have painted arrows overriding the default. On the N2/M50 roundabouts especially, the left lane may be marked left-only, and the middle lane handles straight ahead. Read the road markings early. If the arrows say one thing and the standard rule says another, follow the arrows.

Observation: what the examiner is actually watching

The examiner is checking that you look, not just that you slow down. On approach, they want to see:

  1. A mirror check (centre then right) as you lift off.
  2. A clear look right into the roundabout as you reach the yield line.
  3. A second look if you had to hesitate before moving off.
  4. A left mirror and blind spot check before you change lane on the roundabout or exit.

Rolling up to the yield line staring straight ahead is one of the most common Grade 2 faults at Finglas. Turn your head. Examiners need to see it.

Signalling: the timing rule people get wrong

Signalling too early confuses drivers waiting to enter behind you. Signalling too late means the car behind on the roundabout has no idea you're leaving. The clean pattern:

  • Going left: left signal on well before the yield line, keep it on through the exit.
  • Straight ahead: no signal on approach, left signal as your bonnet passes the exit before yours.
  • Going right: right signal on approach and while on the roundabout, then cancel and signal left as you pass the last exit before yours.

Common marks lost at Finglas roundabouts

  • Straddling lanes on the N2/M50 roundabouts because the entry lane feels wider than it is.
  • Cutting across from the right lane to a left exit without a mirror or shoulder check.
  • Hesitating at a clear yield line, which counts as failing to make progress.
  • No left signal on exit when going straight ahead. Very common, very easy to fix.
  • Late lane change on approach, forcing a rushed indicator and mirror check together.

How to practice before your test

Drive the routes at the same time of day as your test slot. A 9am roundabout at Charlestown is nothing like the same roundabout at 1pm. Do at least two full practice sessions covering the N2/M50 interchange with a sponsor, and rehearse talking your lane choice out loud so the decision becomes automatic.

Other Dublin test centres with tricky roundabouts

If you're testing at more than one centre or comparing options, the roundabout culture varies across the city:

  • Tallaght: heavy multi-lane roundabouts near the Square and N81.
  • Raheny: smaller roundabouts but tight coastal traffic.
  • Dun Laoghaire: fewer big roundabouts, more junction work.
  • Killester: mixed, with some tricky exits off the coast road.
  • Mulhuddart: shares some of the same M50 and N3 corridor roundabouts as Finglas.
