Top 10 Fail Spots in the Finglas Driving Test

Updated 28 May 2026 · DriveFlow editorial

The Finglas test centre on Jamestown Road sends learners onto a mix of busy urban roads, dual carriageways, large multi-lane roundabouts and tight residential estates. It is one of the trickier Dublin test centres because the variation is wide and the traffic is rarely quiet. Below are 10 spots and situations where learners are commonly reported to drop marks or fail outright, with practical fixes for each.

Examiners draw from a local road network, so no two test routes are identical. Use this as a practice map, not a prediction.

1. Pulling out of the test centre onto Jamestown Road

The first few seconds matter. Learners often forget the full mirror sequence (interior, right wing, blind spot) before moving off, or they cut into traffic that is moving faster than expected. Build the moving-off routine into muscle memory so you do not freeze on the day.

2. Jamestown Roundabout (Jamestown Road / Tolka Valley Road)

This roundabout catches a lot of learners. Common mistakes include drifting between lanes, failing to indicate left on exit, and hesitating so long at the yield line that the examiner has to prompt you. Pick your lane early based on your exit, and treat the indicator-off on your exit as a non-negotiable habit.

3. N2 / Finglas Road slip lanes

Joining and leaving the N2 trips learners on observation. The mistake is usually a quick mirror flick without a proper blind-spot check, or merging too slowly and forcing traffic behind to brake. Match the speed of the lane you are joining before you cross the line.

4. The N2 / M50 junction area

You will not be taken on the motorway, but routes regularly skirt the junction. Heavy lane changes, signage clutter and faster speeds catch nervous drivers off guard. Read signs early, commit to your lane, and avoid last-second swerves.

5. Mellowes Road and the Cappagh Road junctions

Several signal-controlled junctions sit close together here. Learners commonly creep over stop lines, react late to amber, or block yellow boxes. Cover the brake earlier than you think you need to, and never enter a box junction unless your exit is clear.

6. The narrow estates off Mellowes Road and Wellmount

These estate roads are tight, often with parked cars on both sides. Failures here usually come from poor positioning (driving down the middle even when oncoming traffic appears), not anticipating reversing cars, or missing a child or pedestrian stepping out. Slow your speed, keep left where safe, and scan driveways and gaps between parked cars constantly.

7. Reverse around a corner in a residential estate

Finglas examiners frequently use quiet estate corners for the reverse. The classic fails are mounting the kerb, finishing too far from the kerb, or never once looking out the back window. Practice the manoeuvre on real corners similar to those around Finglas South and Barry Park, not just empty car parks.

8. Finglas Village and Main Street

Pedestrians, buses pulling in and out, delivery vans double-parked. The fail spots here are usually around the pedestrian crossings and bus stops. Do not creep past a stopped bus without checking for people stepping out, and approach zebra crossings ready to stop, not hoping you can sneak through.

9. Charlestown roundabout area

Multi-lane roundabouts near Charlestown require firm lane discipline. Learners commonly straddle lanes, take the wrong lane for a third or fourth exit, or panic and stop on the roundabout itself. Decide your lane before the give-way line and commit.

10. Returning to the test centre on St Margaret's Road / Jamestown Road

It sounds harsh, but plenty of tests are lost in the last five minutes. Relief makes learners drop concentration: rolling stops at junctions, sloppy mirror checks, and missing a 50 km/h sign as the road character changes. Stay sharp until the handbrake is on and the engine is off in the test centre car park.

How to use this list

Pick two or three spots from this list each time you go out with a sponsor or instructor. Drive them in different conditions: morning rush, midday, and in light rain if you can. The Finglas test is rarely failed on one big mistake; it is usually a build-up of small marks at exactly these locations.
