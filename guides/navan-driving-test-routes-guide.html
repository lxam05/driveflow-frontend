Navan Driving Test Routes: Roads and Areas to Practice

Published 25 September 2026 · DriveFlow

If your RSA test is booked at the Navan centre, knowing the local road network is a big head start. Examiners don't stick to one fixed route, but they draw from a familiar pool of roads around the town, and the patterns are consistent enough that you can practice the exact junctions and estates you'll likely drive on the day.

This guide walks through the corridors, roundabouts, and residential areas that come up most often on Navan tests, plus how to prepare for each.

About the Navan test centre

The Navan test centre sits on the outskirts of town and gives examiners quick access to a mix of urban streets, national roads, and quieter estate driving. Most tests last around 30 to 40 minutes and cover a wide range of speed limits, from 30 km/h estates up to 80 km/h regional stretches. Expect at least one reverse manoeuvre and a hill start, plus questions on the rules of the road and a "show me, tell me" style vehicle check before you set off.

Main corridors examiners commonly use

The Kells Road (N51) direction

Heading west out of Navan on the N51 toward Kells is a classic test corridor. You'll deal with changing speed limits as you leave the built-up area, overtaking parked cars near the edge of town, and merging back into faster-flowing traffic. Practice reading the signs early so you're not braking or accelerating suddenly at each limit change.

The Athboy Road (R154)

The R154 toward Athboy is another regular. It has a rural feel quite quickly, with bends, farm entrances, and slower vehicles you may need to judge. This road tests your ability to hold a safe position, keep a steady speed, and handle situations where overtaking is not appropriate.

The Dublin Road (N3) and Johnstown

Routes heading south toward Johnstown and the N3 involve heavier traffic, filter lanes, and multi-lane junctions. Lane discipline matters here. Get comfortable with committing to the correct lane early, especially on the approach to the larger roundabouts near the Navan retail parks.

The Trim Road (R161)

The Trim Road side of town gives examiners access to a mix of 50 km/h and 60 km/h zones and some sharp junctions where observations really count. Practice pulling out at these T-junctions until you're smooth and confident.

Roundabouts to know

Navan has a lot of roundabouts, and they're one of the most common fail points. Focus your practice on:

  • The larger roundabouts on the ring road and around the retail parks, where lane choice on entry matters as much as observation.
  • Smaller local roundabouts inside estates and on link roads, where late signalling or hesitation causes marks.
  • Any roundabout where you're taking the third or fourth exit. Get the lane, signal, and mirror sequence right every time.

Estates used for reverse and turnabout

Examiners regularly use quiet residential estates within a short drive of the centre to test your reverse around a corner, turnabout (three-point turn), and hill start. Estates in the Johnstown, Windtown, and Blackcastle areas come up often. When you're practicing, look for:

  • Corners with a slight camber where the car will drift if you're not careful.
  • Junctions where parked cars restrict your view.
  • Slight inclines where a hill start under pressure is a real test of clutch control.

Pressure points on test day

The most common places learners drop marks in Navan tend to be:

  1. Roundabout lane choice on the approach to the N3 and retail park areas.
  2. Observation at busy T-junctions on the R161 and R147.
  3. Speed management when transitioning from 50 to 80 zones on the way out of town.
  4. Reverse around a corner in narrow estates with parked cars.
  5. Progress on national roads, driving too slowly is marked just like driving too fast.

How to prepare

Book a few pre-test lessons with an ADI who works around Navan regularly. They'll know the specific junctions and estates examiners favour. On top of that, spend your own practice hours doing loops that combine several route types in one session: town streets, a national road stretch, at least two roundabouts, and an estate for manoeuvres. Repetition on the actual roads is what builds the calm confidence you need on the day.

Also brush up on rules of the road questions, the RSA can and does ask them. You can review the official material at rsa.ie.
