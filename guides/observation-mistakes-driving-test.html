How Examiners Mark Observation Mistakes on the Irish Driving Test

Published 16 July 2026 · DriveFlow Editorial

Observation is the single biggest reason learners fail the driving test in Ireland. It is not that people forget to move the car forward or stall at the lights, it is that they do not show the examiner they have looked. If the examiner cannot see clear head movement, mirrors being checked, or a proper blind spot glance, the fault gets marked, even when the manoeuvre itself was perfectly safe.

This guide explains exactly how RSA examiners grade observation, where they log the fault on the report sheet, and how to build habits that show your checks clearly on test day.

The three grades of fault

Every mistake on your driving test falls into one of three categories on the examiner's marking sheet:

  • Grade 1: A minor fault that is not repeated. This does not fail you.
  • Grade 2: A serious fault, or a Grade 1 fault repeated multiple times. Four or more of these will fail you.
  • Grade 3: A dangerous fault. One is enough to fail the test.

Observation faults are usually marked as Grade 2. That sounds harsh, but the logic is simple: not looking properly is inherently unsafe, even if nothing went wrong that particular time. Repeated missed observations, for example at three separate junctions, will quickly stack up on the marking sheet.

Where observation is marked on the sheet

The examiner's report has specific rows for observation under each section. The most commonly ticked ones are:

  • Moving off: did you check mirrors and blind spot before pulling out?
  • Progress / use of mirrors: did you scan mirrors regularly, before slowing, and before signalling?
  • Turning right / left: did you check the mirror and, where relevant, the blind spot?
  • Roundabouts: did you observe on approach, on the roundabout, and before exiting?
  • Reverse around a corner: did you look through the rear window and check for approaching traffic and pedestrians?
  • Turnabout (three point turn): did you observe in both directions on each phase?
Key point: The examiner is not a mind reader. If your head does not move, the check does not count. Exaggerated, obvious head turns are what you want, not subtle eye flicks.

The most common observation mistakes

1. Mirror before signal, then nothing else

Learners are drilled to do "mirror, signal, manoeuvre" and often stop there. Examiners want to see a mirror check and a blind spot glance before moving off or changing lanes. Missing the blind spot when pulling away from the kerb is a classic Grade 2 fault.

2. Rolling into junctions without a proper look

At give way lines and stop lines, examiners want a clear, deliberate look right, then left, then right again. Creeping forward while still turning your head is fine, freezing and staring straight ahead is not. Busy urban centres like Tallaght, Finglas, and Raheny have layered junctions where this gets caught every day.

3. No mirror before slowing down

Before you brake for a hazard, a parked car, or a turn, the examiner expects a rear mirror check. If a motorbike is filtering behind you and you brake without looking, that is a serious fault regardless of what actually happens.

4. Roundabouts, especially the exit

Approach observation is usually fine because learners are focused on gaps. The mistake tends to come on the roundabout: no mirror check and no blind spot glance before exiting. This is the number one fail point at centres like Naas, Carlow, and Navan where multi-lane roundabouts are unavoidable.

5. Reversing with tunnel vision

During the reverse around a corner, examiners want to see you scanning: rear window, both side mirrors, and forward through the windscreen. Fixing your eyes only out the back window is a fault, even if the reverse itself is neat.

How to make your observation visible

  • Turn your whole head. Chin to shoulder is the standard for blind spot checks.
  • Mirror-signal-mirror-manoeuvre. Add that second mirror check after signalling, before you actually move.
  • Talk yourself through it during practice. Saying "mirror, blind spot, going" out loud during lessons builds the habit.
  • Scan every 5 to 8 seconds. A quick centre mirror check while driving straight shows the examiner you are aware of what is behind you.
  • Look before you brake, not while you brake.

Practicing observation before test day

Observation habits are built through repetition, not through reading. Spend a few full lessons focused only on head movement and mirror timing. Ask whoever is supervising you to sit in the passenger seat and watch your head, not the road, so they can flag every missed check.

It is also worth doing this on the actual roads around your test centre. Every centre has its own rhythm of junctions, roundabouts, and lane changes. Practicing the specific routes gives you a chance to build the observation reflex in the exact spots the examiner will be watching.
