Top 10 Fail Spots in the Mulhuddart Driving Test

Updated September 2026

Mulhuddart is one of the busiest test centres in west Dublin, and the test area covers a mix of housing estates, dual carriageways, big multi-lane roundabouts, and short slip roads onto the N3. That variety is exactly why learners come unstuck here. The examiner can pull in almost anything within about 15 minutes of the centre, so you need to be comfortable across the whole patch, not just the quiet estate near the door.

Below are 10 spots and situations that commonly catch learners out on the Mulhuddart test, along with how to handle each one on the day. For the full picture of routes and practice videos, see the Mulhuddart routes page.

1. The Coolmine Roundabout on the N3

This large multi-lane roundabout links Clonsilla Road, the N3, and Blanchardstown. Learners often fail here for late lane selection, straddling lanes, or hesitating on entry. Read the overhead signs early, commit to a lane well before the give-way line, and keep steady progress. Sitting and waiting for a huge gap will get you marked for undue hesitation.

2. Snugborough Road interchange

The Snugborough interchange near Blanchardstown Shopping Centre has a rapid sequence of roundabouts and slip roads. Common fails include drifting lanes on the roundabout, missing a lane-change mirror check, or slowing too much on the slip. Practice reading the road layout as one continuous manoeuvre, not three separate ones.

3. Merging onto the N3 southbound

Short slip roads are a classic Mulhuddart fail spot. Learners either crawl on and force N3 traffic to brake, or floor it without a mirror check. Build to the flow of traffic, take a firm right-shoulder check, and merge decisively into a gap.

4. The Ongar Distributor Road roundabouts

Heading out toward Ongar and Hansfield, you will meet a chain of roundabouts with cyclists, buses, and school-run traffic. The frequent fault here is signalling late on the exit or not signalling at all when leaving. Left indicator goes on as you pass the exit before yours.

5. Clonsilla Road bus lanes

Bus lane hours confuse learners. Driving in a live bus lane, or swerving out of one at the last second, is a serious fault. Check the sign at the start of the lane, know when it is in operation, and plan your position early.

6. Church Road, Mulhuddart village

The narrow stretch through the village has parked cars, pedestrian crossings, and oncoming traffic that has to filter around obstructions. Learners fail here for poor positioning and not giving way when it is clearly their turn to yield. Slow, look, decide, and communicate with other drivers.

7. Estate driving around Tyrrelstown and Hollystown

Examiners love the housing estates off the Ratoath Road for observation and speed control. Common faults are rolling through junctions without a proper stop, going over 30 km/h on straight estate roads, and missing children or cyclists at side turns. Treat every side road as a potential hazard.

8. The reverse around a corner on a side road

Manoeuvres are usually done in the quieter estates near Damastown or Tyrrelstown. The reverse fails often come from clipping the kerb, going too wide, or not doing enough all-round observation. Keep it slow, look out the back window, and check the front nearside as the car swings.

9. Damastown Industrial Estate junctions

The wide, quiet roads in Damastown feel easy, but that is where learners drop their concentration. Rolling stops at unmarked junctions and creeping over yield lines are the usual issues. Every give-way and stop line still counts, even when there is nothing coming.

10. Return to the test centre on Church Road

The final approach back to the centre is not a formality. Learners have failed here for a rushed left turn into the car park, poor mirror use, or coasting in neutral. Stay in gear, mirror-signal-manoeuvre as normal, and park tidily inside the lines.

Final word

Mulhuddart is not an unfair test centre, but it demands that you handle a proper mix of environments in one 30 minute drive. Examiners are looking for consistent observation, steady progress, and confident lane discipline on the bigger junctions. Spend your practice hours on the N3 slips, Coolmine and Snugborough roundabouts, and the Tyrrelstown estates rather than the same familiar loop near your house.
