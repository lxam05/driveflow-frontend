Top 10 Fail Spots in the Cork Wilton Driving Test

Updated 4 October 2026 · DriveFlow

Cork Wilton is one of the busier test centres in Munster, and the examiner routes pull in a mix of suburban estate roads, the N40 slip roads, and tight urban junctions around Bishopstown and Glasheen. Learners sitting the test here often lose marks in the same handful of places. Below are 10 fail spots that come up again and again in reports from candidates who sat the test at Wilton, and what you can do to clean each one up before test day.

For the full route breakdown and practice maps, see our Cork Wilton test routes guide.

1. Sarsfield Road roundabout

This roundabout sits right beside the test centre and almost every route touches it. Learners commonly fail here for late lane changes, drifting across lanes on exit, or hesitating in the right lane when they should be committing. Practice approaching in the correct lane well before the give-way line, and keep a steady speed on exit without straddling the white lines.

2. Bishopstown Avenue junctions

The side roads off Bishopstown Avenue have poor sightlines and parked cars tucked right up to the junction. Examiners often watch how you edge out at these T-junctions. Candidates get marked for either rolling out too far without looking right properly, or stopping so far back they cannot see at all. Creep forward in small, controlled movements with full observation each time.

3. The N40 slip road at Bishopstown

Joining the N40 is a classic Wilton fail spot. The slip is short, traffic is often moving at 100 km/h, and learners either crawl onto the main carriageway or sit braking in the slip. Build your speed on the slip, check your right mirror and blind spot early, and merge decisively into a gap. Hesitation here is one of the quickest ways to collect a grade 3.

4. Model Farm Road

Model Farm Road has a 50 km/h limit but it is wide and quiet, so learners drift up to 60. Speed control is heavily marked across the Wilton routes. Keep an eye on your speedo on this stretch, especially downhill towards the Wilton roundabout.

5. Wilton Roundabout (Dennehy's Cross area)

Multi-lane roundabouts near the Wilton Shopping Centre catch out learners who are unsure which lane to take for the third exit. Not signalling left as you pass your second exit is a very common mark. Plan your lane on approach, and remember to indicate left when the exit before yours goes by.

6. Glasheen Road / College Road area

These residential roads near UCC are narrow, with parked cars on both sides and plenty of cyclists and pedestrians. Learners often fail for not giving enough clearance when passing parked cars, or for not slowing when a cyclist is ahead. Treat every parked car as a potential hazard and give at least a metre of space where you can.

7. Estate roads off Curraheen

Examiners regularly dip into residential estates around Curraheen and Bishopstown for the reverse around a corner or to test observation at unmarked junctions. Learners commonly forget that unmarked crossroads require full observation and reduced speed. Do not assume you have right of way just because there is no sign.

8. Pedestrian crossings on Wilton Road

There are several zebra and signalised crossings along Wilton Road near the shopping centre. Failing to anticipate pedestrians waiting, or stopping too late, picks up marks under observation and reaction. Scan the footpath well in advance and cover the brake when you see anyone near a crossing.

9. The hill start on Magazine Road area

Hill starts and moving off on a gradient come up often around the Glasheen and Magazine Road hills. Rolling back more than a car length, or stalling because the clutch comes up too fast, are both marked. Practice holding the car on the clutch and gentle gas before releasing the handbrake smoothly.

10. Returning to the test centre car park

It sounds minor, but learners often relax on the way back in and lose marks on the final approach: wrong signalling into the Wilton centre, late mirror checks, or a sloppy park. Treat the last two minutes with the same focus as the first. The test is not over until the handbrake is on and the engine is off.

Practice these spots before test day. Our Cork Wilton routes cover the most commonly reported examiner loops, including Bishopstown, the N40 slips, Model Farm Road and the estates around Curraheen.

How to use this list

Pick two or three fail spots per practice session rather than trying to tackle them all at once. Drive each one in both directions, at different times of day, so you see the traffic patterns a real test might throw at you. If you are doing EDT lessons, ask your instructor to focus specifically on the Sarsfield Road roundabout and the N40 merge, since those two account for a large share of grade 3 marks at Wilton.
