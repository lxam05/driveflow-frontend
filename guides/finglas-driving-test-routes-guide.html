Finglas Driving Test Routes: Roads, Junctions and Manoeuvres

Updated August 2026

The Finglas test centre is one of Dublin's busiest, and the roads around it reflect that. You'll deal with steady traffic, tight residential estates, complex junctions on the N2 corridor, and roundabouts that catch out learners who haven't put in enough local practice. This guide walks you through the roads, junctions and manoeuvres you're most likely to face on test day, and how to prepare for them.

Where you start: the Finglas test centre area

Finglas test centre sits off Jamestown Road, close to the N2 and within easy reach of the Finglas Village area, Ballygall, Glasnevin and Cappagh. Examiners typically draw from a network of roads that mix residential 50 km/h streets with faster national road sections and a few busy roundabouts. There isn't one fixed route, but the corridors are consistent enough that learners who practice locally have a real advantage.

Key roads you should know

  • Jamestown Road and Ballygall Road: the usual starting stretch. Expect parked cars narrowing the carriageway, side road junctions on both sides, and pedestrians near shops and schools.
  • N2 / Finglas Road: a national road with heavier traffic and higher speed limits in sections. You need to hold your lane confidently and time your merges and exits properly.
  • McKee Avenue and Cappagh Road: longer estate roads with sharper bends, hidden junctions and traffic-calming features.
  • Mellowes Road and Wellmount Road: tighter residential streets often used for the reverse around a corner manoeuvre.
  • North Road (R135): higher-speed dual sections and lane discipline are tested here.

Junctions that catch learners out

Finglas has a handful of junctions where nerves and hesitation cost marks. Examiners aren't looking for perfection, they're looking for observation, decision-making and control.

  • Jamestown Road onto the N2: a busy junction where poor observation and slow decision-making are common fail reasons.
  • Ballygall Road / Glasnevin Avenue: multiple entry points and often heavy traffic. Practice lane positioning and clear signalling here.
  • Finglas Village crossroads: pedestrian crossings, buses stopping, and cyclists all demand strong mirror checks.
  • Cappagh Road roundabouts: examiners want to see you signal on exit and hold the correct lane through.

Roundabouts around Finglas

Roundabouts are the single most common pressure point in Dublin tests, and Finglas is no exception. You'll likely meet at least one on your route. The core rules are simple but easily forgotten under pressure: approach in the correct lane, indicate on entry only if turning left or right, signal left before your exit, and check your left mirror and blind spot as you leave. If you're going straight ahead, no entry signal, but you must still signal left as you pass the exit before yours.

Manoeuvres you should have nailed

The RSA examiner will pick from a set of standard manoeuvres. In Finglas, the quiet estate roads off Wellmount, Mellowes and parts of Ballygall are the typical settings. Be ready for:

  • Reverse around a corner to the left, ideally on a road with a gentle kerb line and moderate width.
  • Turnabout (three-point turn) on a residential road, watching for driveways and parked cars.
  • Hill start, which crops up on some of the sloped side streets in the area.
  • Parking, either between two vehicles or on the left, with proper observation.

Full observation is what separates passes from fails here. Look properly, don't just glance.

How to prepare for a Finglas test

  • Do at least three practice drives around the test centre in different traffic conditions, including morning rush and mid-afternoon.
  • Drive the N2 both directions so higher-speed lane changes feel natural.
  • Practice at least two different roundabouts locally, focusing on lane discipline and exit signalling.
  • Reverse around a corner on at least three different streets so it isn't tied to one specific kerb.
  • Review the RSA rules of the road, especially the sections on observation and roundabouts.
