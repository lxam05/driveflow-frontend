Top 10 Fail Spots in the Naas Driving Test

Updated 22 August 2026 · DriveFlow editorial team

Naas test centre sits just off the Dublin Road, and examiners draw from a compact local network that mixes tight town streets, busy retail-park roundabouts, and faster national roads. The same handful of spots come up again and again in learner debriefs. Below are the 10 areas where Naas candidates most commonly lose marks, along with what tends to go wrong and how to handle each one.

1. Monread Road roundabout

This is arguably the most talked-about junction in the Naas route network. Learners often approach in the wrong lane, hesitate at the give-way line, or drift across lane markings on exit. Practice reading the overhead signs early, commit to your lane well before the roundabout, and keep your indicators clean (right on approach if you're going past 12 o'clock, left as you pass the exit before yours).

2. Dublin Road merge near the test centre

Right at the doorstep of the centre, learners rush the pull-out, fail to check the right blind spot, or accelerate too slowly into a national speed-limit zone. Take a proper mirror-signal-mirror-blindspot routine, then match the flow of traffic. Nervous under-acceleration here is a common mark for "progress".

3. Newbridge Road (R445) speed changes

The R445 out towards Newbridge changes limits several times. Learners either sit at 60 in an 80 zone (undue hesitation) or carry 80 into a 60 stretch. Read every speed-limit sign aloud in your head during practice runs, and adjust smoothly rather than braking late.

4. Ballymore Eustace Road narrow sections

Heading south of Naas, the road narrows with hedgerows tight to the verge. Common faults are hugging the centre line, over-braking for oncoming traffic, or swinging wide on bends. Keep a steady position roughly a metre from the left edge and slow early for blind bends rather than mid-corner.

5. Sallins Road and the level crossing area

Traffic queues near Sallins can catch learners out. Stopping across yellow box junctions, poor clutch control on the incline, or missing pedestrians near the pathways are all commonly reported. Leave a clear gap in front, check for pedestrians at every crossing, and use the handbrake if you're stationary for more than a moment.

6. Millennium Park estate roads

Examiners regularly use the industrial and business estates around Millennium Park for the reverse-around-a-corner or turnabout. Learners fail here by mounting kerbs, not observing over both shoulders during the manoeuvre, or picking a corner with a dropped kerb (driveway) by mistake. Slow, controlled clutch work and full observations win marks here.

7. Naas town centre one-way system

The Main Street and South Main Street one-ways trip learners up with lane discipline, box junctions, and pedestrians stepping out. Stay in the correct lane for your intended exit, don't block junctions, and cover the brake around pedestrian areas without riding it.

8. Blessington Road (R410) junction

The junction turning onto or off the R410 tends to be busy, and learners either edge out too far at the yield or freeze when a gap appears. Practice reading gaps in traffic and committing decisively once it's safe. Creeping without observing is a common serious fault.

9. Traffic-light junctions on the Sallins bypass

Filter lanes and green arrows here catch out learners who don't know when to go on a green filter versus a full green. Learn the difference, keep both hands ready on the wheel at lights, and don't roll forward before your signal is fully green.

10. Return approach to the test centre

Believe it or not, the last two minutes back into the centre car park is where relaxed candidates lose marks. Common faults include failing to indicate into the entrance, poor mirror checks as you slow, and a sloppy final park. Treat the last stretch like the first, mirrors, signal, position, speed, until the handbrake is on and the engine is off.

How to prepare for these Naas fail spots

The best defence is targeted practice on the actual roads examiners use. Drive the Monread and Millennium Park areas at different times of day, run the Newbridge and Ballymore roads for speed-change discipline, and rehearse your manoeuvres in a quiet estate off the Sallins Road. Booked and cancelled slots move quickly, so use the wait for real practice, not just theory revision.
