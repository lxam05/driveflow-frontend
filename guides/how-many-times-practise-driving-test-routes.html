How Many Times Should You Practice Your Driving Test Routes?

Updated 25 October 2026 · DriveFlow

Short answer: drive every main road around your test centre at least three to five times before test day, and the trickier junctions, roundabouts, and estates closer to eight or ten. That is enough to stop thinking about the road and start thinking about your observation, mirrors, and speed.

Most learners who fail the RSA test do not fail because they cannot drive. They fail because they are driving somewhere unfamiliar under pressure. Practicing the actual roads an examiner is likely to use removes that pressure almost entirely.

Why route practice matters more than hours in the car

The RSA does not publish exact test routes, but examiners draw from a local road network around each test centre. That network does not change often. The same roundabouts, the same tricky slip roads, the same narrow estate roads come up again and again.

If you have driven a roundabout ten times, you already know which lane you need, when to indicate, and where traffic tends to appear from. If you have never seen it before, you are reading signs mid-manoeuvre while the examiner is marking your sheet. That is the difference between a pass and a fail on a single junction.

A realistic practice schedule

Here is a structure that works for most learners in the four to six weeks before the test:

Weeks 1 to 2: Learn the network

  • Drive each main corridor out of the test centre at least twice.
  • Cover every roundabout within a 10 minute drive of the centre.
  • Drive at least two or three estate loops used for the reverse around a corner.

Weeks 3 to 4: Repeat and tighten

  • Repeat each full route three to five times.
  • Focus on the sections you felt unsure about, not the ones you already know.
  • Practice at the same time of day as your test slot so the traffic feels right.

Final week: Simulate the test

  • Do two or three full dry runs with no interruptions, as if the examiner is in the car.
  • Start and finish at the test centre car park, including the pull in and pull out.
  • Finish with manoeuvres practice in a quiet nearby estate.

That gives you roughly 20 to 30 route drives in total. Any less and the roads still feel new. Any more and you are usually just burning fuel.

Popular centres learners practice most

Some centres have much tighter road networks and trickier junction patterns, so route repetition matters even more. If you are testing at any of these, aim for the higher end of the practice range:

  • Tallaght and Finglas, where multi-lane roundabouts catch learners out.
  • Raheny and Killester, with narrow residential sections and busy main roads.
  • Dun Laoghaire, where coastal junctions and one-way systems need repetition.
  • Naas and Navan, which mix town centre work with faster national roads.
  • Wilton and Ballincollig around Cork, where roundabout clusters trip up a lot of candidates.
  • Carnmore and Clybaun in Galway, with a mix of rural N roads and estate work.

What to focus on during each repeat

Driving the same road five times only helps if you are improving something each time. On every repeat, pick one focus:

  • Observation: mirror checks before every signal, lane change, and slowdown.
  • Speed: are you reading the signs early, especially 50 to 80 transitions?
  • Positioning: correct lane well before the roundabout, not at the last second.
  • Progress: are you holding people up unnecessarily, especially on national roads?

When to stop practicing

You are ready when the roads feel boring. If you can drive a full loop around your test centre and describe aloud what is coming at the next three junctions without checking signs, you have practiced enough. Add one or two refresher drives in the final two days and go in rested.
