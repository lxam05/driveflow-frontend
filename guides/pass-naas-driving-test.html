How to Pass the Naas Driving Test: Local Tips That Actually Work

Updated 23 August 2026 · DriveFlow

The Naas test centre sits just off the Kilcullen Road, and its catchment covers a mix of busy town streets, fast national roads, and the kind of small housing estates examiners love for reverse manoeuvres. If you know what to expect from the local road network, you can walk in far more relaxed than most learners. Here is what actually matters on the day.

Know the Naas road network before test day

Examiners in Naas draw from a well-established set of local roads. There is no single fixed route, but the same corridors come up again and again. Spend your practice hours on these:

  • The Kilcullen Road (R448) heading south out of town. Speed limits change quickly here, and missing a 50 to 80 transition (or the drop back into 60) is one of the most common grade 3 faults recorded at this centre.
  • The Ballymore Eustace Road. Narrow in sections, bends without warning, and often has oncoming traffic that requires early positioning and steady speed control.
  • The Monread and Sallins Road areas. Expect roundabouts, pedestrian crossings, and cyclists.
  • Estates off Craddockstown Road, Rathasker, and Oldtown. These are the go-to spots for the reverse around a corner and turnabout.

What Naas examiners focus on

Every RSA examiner works from the same marking sheet, but experienced instructors in Kildare will tell you the Naas cohort tends to be strict on a few specific areas:

1. Roundabout discipline

Naas is roundabout country. The junction at the Newbridge Road, the Monread roundabout, and the ones leading toward the M7 all come up regularly. Examiners want to see you signalling on approach, choosing the correct lane early, and signalling left before your exit. Sitting in the wrong lane at a two-lane roundabout is an instant grade 3.

2. Speed-limit awareness

The transitions from 50 to 80 to 100 (and back down) happen fast on routes out toward Kilcullen and Ballymore Eustace. Sitting at 60 in an 80 zone is marked as "progress" and will fail you just as quickly as speeding. Make progress when it is safe.

3. Observation at junctions

The junction from the R445 onto the Newbridge Road, and several unmarked estate exits, need proper left-right-left checks. Rolling up without a clear stop and look is the single most common reason learners fail here.

4. Position on narrow roads

The country lanes south and west of Naas are tight. Drifting toward the centre line, or hugging the verge unnecessarily, gets marked under "position normal driving".

Preparing in the weeks before

Your last few weeks should not be about learning new skills. They should be about repetition on Naas roads specifically. A good plan looks like this:

  • Complete your EDT lessons well before the test date, not the week of.
  • Book at least two lessons with an instructor who teaches in Naas regularly. They know which estates examiners use.
  • Practice all four manoeuvres (reverse around a corner, turnabout, hill start, and parking) in real Naas estates, not empty car parks.
  • Drive the likely test corridors at the same time of day as your test slot. Traffic on the Sallins Road at 9am is a different world to 2pm.
  • Do a mock test the week before with your instructor. Ask for a full marking sheet.

Test-day mindset

Arrive 20 minutes early. Bring your learner permit (originals, not photocopies) and confirm your car meets the RSA requirements: L plates, valid NCT, tax, insurance, and working indicators and brake lights. Examiners will refuse to conduct a test if any of these are missing.

During the test, treat every instruction as a normal drive. If you make a small mistake, do not dwell on it. Grade 2 faults do not fail you on their own. Panicking and stacking three more grade 2s in the next minute will.

Talk yourself through commentary if it helps: "checking mirrors, signalling, moving off". Examiners are not marking you on silence. They are marking your observation, control, and decision-making.

After the test

If you pass, congratulations. If you do not, ask the examiner to walk you through the marking sheet before you leave the centre. The specific fault locations are gold for your next attempt. Book your retest, focus on the exact areas flagged, and you will be back in a much stronger position.

The Naas test is fair. It rewards learners who have put in real hours on real Kildare roads, and it punishes those who have only driven a handful of quiet estates. Prepare properly, drive the routes that come up, and give yourself the best possible chance.

Good luck.
