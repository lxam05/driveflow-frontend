Top 10 Fail Spots in the Galway Westside Driving Test

Updated 10 October 2026 · DriveFlow Editorial

The Galway Westside test centre sits in a busy part of the city, with examiners drawing on a local road network that mixes residential estates, tight junctions, and some of the trickiest roundabouts in the west. Routes vary, but certain stretches come up again and again, and the same mistakes get marked down week after week. Below are the top 10 fail spots commonly reported by Westside candidates, and what you can do to stay out of trouble.

1. Seamus Quirke Road at the Westside shops

This stretch is busy with buses, cyclists, and pedestrians stepping out between parked cars. Learners often fail here for weak observation when moving off, or for drifting too close to parked vehicles. Keep your scan active, check the right blind spot every time, and leave a full door's width of clearance.

2. The Kirwan Roundabout (N6/N59 junction)

Kirwan is a large, multi-lane roundabout where lane discipline falls apart under pressure. Learners commonly straddle lanes or signal too late when exiting. Pick your lane well before the roundabout based on your exit, signal left as you pass the exit before yours, and do not change lanes once you're on it.

3. The Browne Roundabout

Smaller than Kirwan but equally punishing. Candidates often creep out when the right-hand lane isn't actually clear, or hesitate when it is. Treat it as a full stop line, look right properly, and go decisively when you have the gap.

4. Rahoon Road junction with the Western Distributor Road

This junction mixes queuing traffic with a quick-moving main road. Learners often stall under pressure or pull out into gaps that are too small. Practice judging gaps on a national-speed road, and remember progress is marked too, so don't sit at a clear junction.

5. Estate driving around Knocknacarra

Estates like Cnoc na Cathrach and Gleann na Ri are regularly used for turnabouts and reversing manoeuvres. Fails here are usually for mounting the kerb, missing a child or cyclist, or forgetting observations during the turn. Full 360 degree checks before and during every manoeuvre, and never rely on mirrors alone.

6. Threadneedle Road school zone

If your test runs during school hours, expect pedestrians, double-parked cars, and 30 km/h signage. Learners often miss the speed-limit change or fail to anticipate someone stepping out. Cover the brake, drop your speed early, and read the road ahead.

7. Bishop O'Donnell Road merges

This dual-carriageway section catches people out when joining or changing lanes. Common fails include merging without a proper blind-spot check, or matching the speed of traffic too late. Build speed on the slip, pick your gap early, and commit.

8. The Taylor's Hill area

Narrow, tree-lined roads with on-street parking and a mix of priorities. Candidates often fail for poor positioning or for not giving way where oncoming traffic has priority through a parked-car pinch point. Hang back, make eye contact if you can, and don't force your way through.

9. Salthill Promenade area turns

Routes occasionally dip toward Salthill, where tourists, cyclists, and jay-walkers add unpredictability. Fails here come from late reactions and poor speed control. Keep well below the limit when pedestrians are near the kerb, and expect the unexpected.

10. The return approach to the test centre

Confidence drops just as the test ends, and so does concentration. Learners pick up late marks on the final roundabouts and the junction back into the centre by rolling stop lines or skipping the final mirror check before pulling in. Stay switched on until the handbrake is on and the engine is off.

Preparing for your Westside test

None of these fail spots are impossible, but they do reward specific, local practice. Drive the actual roads at the time of day your test is booked for, so you see the real traffic conditions.
