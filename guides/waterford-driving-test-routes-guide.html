Waterford Driving Test Routes: What to Expect

Updated 16 October 2026 · DriveFlow

If your test is booked at the Waterford test centre on the Cork Road, this guide walks you through the kinds of roads and junctions you are likely to meet. The RSA does not publish exact test routes, and examiners draw from a local road network with regular variation, but patterns do emerge once you spend time practicing around the area. Here is what learners typically report after sitting the test in Waterford city.

Where the Waterford test centre is

The test centre sits off the Cork Road (R680) on the western side of the city, close to the Waterford Institute of Technology campus and the Six Cross Roads business park. That location shapes almost every route. You can expect to leave the centre onto the Cork Road and head either toward the city or outward toward the Outer Ring Road (N25) within the first few minutes.

Because the surrounding area mixes busy national road, suburban estates, and city traffic, examiners can test a wide range of skills without covering huge distances. That is good news if you prepare the right areas thoroughly.

Common route corridors

From spending time around the centre, you can expect your route to pull from several familiar corridors. Nothing here is guaranteed, but these are the areas learners mention again and again.

Cork Road and the Outer Ring Road (N25)

The Cork Road itself is a key corridor. You will likely drive sections of it at some point, dealing with changing speed limits, bus lanes near WIT, pedestrian crossings, and lane discipline where it widens. Many routes also feed onto the Outer Ring Road via the roundabouts at Six Cross Roads or further toward Kilbarry. The N25 brings dual-carriageway driving, higher speeds, and the challenge of merging and exiting cleanly.

Ardkeen and the Dunmore Road

Toward the east of the city, Ardkeen and the Dunmore Road (R684) are commonly used. Expect busy traffic near the hospital, retail parks around Ardkeen, and a run of roundabouts and signal-controlled junctions that test your observation and lane choice. Changes between 50 km/h and 60 km/h zones along this stretch catch out learners who are not watching the signs.

City-side routes toward Ballybricken and the quays

Some tests head inward toward Ballybricken, the one-way system around John Street, or the quays along the River Suir. These bring tighter streets, cyclists, buses, and parked cars narrowing lanes. If you have only practiced on open roads, this is often where confidence drops.

Estate driving

Reversing around a corner is usually set up in a quieter residential estate. Areas off the Cork Road, around Hennessy's Road, Lismore Park, and estates near WIT are often used. Practice reverses in a few different estates rather than the same one every time, because camber, kerb height, and junction angles vary.

Pressure points on the Waterford test

  • Six Cross Roads and Outer Ring roundabouts. Lane choice on approach, signalling on exit, and keeping pace with traffic are frequent fail points.
  • Speed limit changes on the Cork Road. Zones shift as you move between the N25, suburban stretches, and the WIT area. Driving too slowly for the limit is marked just as readily as speeding.
  • Dunmore Road junctions near Ardkeen. Multiple lanes, filter arrows, and heavy traffic reward learners who plan their lane early.
  • City one-way system. Clear observation at every junction and confidence pulling out into gaps.
  • Hill starts. Waterford has plenty of mild gradients. Expect at least one controlled stop on a slope.

How to prepare for the Waterford test

Spend lesson time on the exact roads you are likely to meet. A few focused sessions on the Cork Road, Outer Ring, Dunmore Road, and a loop through the quays and Ballybricken is worth more than hours in unfamiliar areas. Mix in several estates for the reverse and ask your sponsor or instructor to simulate independent driving by naming a destination and letting you plan the route.

Check the RSA site for current rules on documents, eyesight, and car requirements before the day. Arrive at the centre at least 15 minutes early, and if possible drive a short warm-up loop around the Cork Road area to settle any nerves.
