Irish Driving Test Checklist 2026: What to Bring and How to Prepare

Updated June 2026 · DriveFlow Editorial

If your RSA driving test is coming up in 2026, the last thing you want is to be turned away at the door for something silly like an expired learner permit or a missing N-plate. This checklist walks you through everything you need before, on, and during test day, written specifically for Irish learners sitting the test at any of the RSA test centres around the country.

Print it, screenshot it, or work through it the night before. Either way, tick every item off before you leave the house.

Quick summary: Bring your learner permit, make sure your car is taxed, insured, and NCT-valid, have L-plates front and back, and arrive at least 15 minutes early. The full breakdown is below.

1. Before Test Day (1–2 weeks out)

Most fails happen because of habits formed weeks before, not nerves on the day. Use this window to fix the small stuff.

  • Confirm your test booking on the RSA website. Double-check the centre, date, and time.
  • Check your learner permit is in date on test day. An expired permit means an automatic refusal.
  • Complete all 12 EDT lessons and make sure your logbook is signed by your ADI.
  • Book a pre-test lesson in the car you'll sit the test in, ideally on routes near your centre.
  • Study the actual test area. Examiners draw from a local road network with variation, so practicing the real roads matters. See the route guides for Tallaght, Finglas, Naas, Raheny, Wilton (Cork), and Westside Galway.
  • Practice the technical checks. You'll be asked to identify items under the bonnet (oil, coolant, brake fluid, power steering, windscreen washer) and explain warning lights.
  • Read the latest "Rules of the Road" for any 2026 updates, especially around speed limits and vulnerable road users.

2. The Night Before

  • Charge your phone and set two alarms.
  • Lay out your learner permit (plastic card) where you can see it.
  • Check the weather forecast and plan clothing and shoes (flat soles, not chunky boots).
  • Plan your route to the test centre and add at least 20 minutes buffer for traffic and parking.
  • Get to bed early. Cramming roundabout theory at midnight will not help.

3. Documents Checklist

If any of these are missing, the test will not go ahead and your fee may be forfeited.

  • ✅ Current learner permit (plastic card, in date)
  • ✅ Test booking confirmation (email or printed)
  • ✅ Glasses or contact lenses, if required on your permit

4. Car Checklist

The examiner will refuse to sit into a car that isn't roadworthy. Walk around it before you leave.

  • ✅ Valid motor tax displayed
  • ✅ Valid NCT (if the car is 4+ years old)
  • ✅ Valid insurance covering you as a learner
  • ✅ L-plates front and back, clearly visible (not in the rear window curled up)
  • ✅ All lights working: headlights, brake lights, indicators, fog lights, reverse light
  • ✅ Tyres legal (1.6mm tread minimum) and properly inflated, including the spare
  • ✅ Windscreen clean, no chips in the driver's line of sight, wipers and washer working
  • ✅ Seatbelts functioning, no warning lights on the dash
  • ✅ Mirrors intact and adjustable
  • ✅ Horn working
  • ✅ Fuel: at least a quarter tank. Running out mid-test is an instant fail.

5. On Test Day Morning

  • Eat something light. A proper breakfast steadies the nerves.
  • Avoid energy drinks. Caffeine makes your hands shake on roundabouts.
  • Drive to the centre with your sponsor or instructor. Do a 20-minute warm-up loop nearby.
  • Arrive 15 minutes before your slot. Late arrivals are usually refused.
  • Bring your documents to reception. The examiner will collect you from the waiting area.

6. During the Test

Once you're moving, the basics decide the result. Keep these front of mind:

  • Mirrors, signal, manoeuvre for every change of direction or speed.
  • Check blind spots when moving off and changing lanes. Missed observation is the single biggest fail cause.
  • Stop fully at stop signs. A rolling stop is a grade 3 fault.
  • Use the correct lane on roundabouts and signal on the way out. If your centre is in Dublin, study the common fail patterns before going in.
  • Mind your speed in changing limits, especially leaving estates onto 50 or 60 km/h roads.
  • Don't argue marks if the examiner queries something. Stay calm and keep driving.

7. After the Test

  • You'll get the result at the centre along with a report sheet listing any faults.
  • If you pass, you can apply for your first full licence at the NDLS.
  • If you don't pass, read the report carefully, book a few targeted lessons on the weak areas, and rebook through the RSA.

Final Tip

The biggest gains in 2026 come from practicing the actual roads your examiner uses. Pick your centre from the DriveFlow routes list or browse the test centres hub, then spend a few hours covering the real junctions, roundabouts, and estates before your slot. You'll walk in knowing what to expect.

Good luck. You've got this.
