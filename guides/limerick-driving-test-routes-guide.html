Limerick Driving Test Routes: Roads and Areas to Practice

Updated October 2026 · DriveFlow

If you're sitting your test at the Limerick centre in Castlemungret, you want to know exactly where you'll be driving. Examiners don't release official route maps, but the roads around the centre are limited, and learners who sit tests here report the same corridors coming up again and again. This guide walks you through the typical areas, the junctions that catch people out, and how to structure your practice so test day feels familiar.

Where is the Limerick test centre?

The Limerick driving test centre is based at Castlemungret, on the southwest side of the city off the R526 Dock Road area. It serves a wide catchment, so wait times can stretch out and the test routes themselves pull in a mix of city driving, estate roads, and faster national roads. Typical route corridors

Routes commonly head out of Castlemungret and loop through a combination of the following areas. You should feel comfortable driving each of these in both directions and at different times of day.

Raheen and the Raheen Business Park

Raheen comes up often. The business park has a mix of mini roundabouts, pedestrian crossings, and tight junctions where lane discipline matters. Dooradoyle Road, the Crescent Shopping Centre area, and the surrounding housing estates all feature. Watch for buses pulling out around the shopping centre and parents on the school run.

Dooradoyle and Ballykeeffe

Expect residential 50 km/h zones, speed ramps, and parked cars narrowing the road. These estates test your ability to progress safely without hesitating, while still giving way to oncoming traffic at pinch points.

Mungret village and the N69

Mungret has grown massively in recent years, with new estates feeding onto the old road network. You may be asked to drive sections of the N69 towards Clarina, which brings 80 km/h and 100 km/h national road driving into play. Observation on entry and exit, correct gear selection, and holding a steady position in the lane are what examiners look for here.

Ballinacurra and the South Circular Road

Closer to the city, routes can push into Ballinacurra, the South Circular, and the Punches Cross area. This is where you'll meet busier urban traffic, traffic-light junctions, and the kind of multi-lane decisions that need early planning.

Roundabouts and junctions that catch learners out

  • Raheen Roundabout: Multi-lane, busy, and often where lane choice goes wrong. Know your exits before you commit.
  • Dock Road junctions: Fast-moving traffic joining from the right. Hesitation here loses marks quickly.
  • Mungret estate exits: Many give onto roads with limited sight lines. Creep, look properly both ways, and don't roll out early.
  • Punches Cross: Complex signalised junction with cyclists and multiple turning lanes. Early positioning is everything.

Manoeuvres and what to expect inside the test

Examiners at Limerick typically ask for a reverse around a corner or a turnabout on quieter residential roads in Raheen or Mungret. The hill start is often done on one of the gentle inclines near the centre. You may also get a few technical checks under the bonnet and a short set of secondary control questions before you set off.

How to structure your practice

  • Spend at least two sessions driving in and out of Castlemungret so the start of the test feels routine.
  • Practice Raheen Roundabout in both quiet and busy conditions. Lane choice is muscle memory.
  • Drive the N69 section at least once a week. National road confidence is often where city-based learners drop marks.
  • Do a full mock test with your sponsor or instructor using a looped Mungret and Raheen route of around 30 to 35 minutes.
  • Review the official RSA guidance at rsa.ie for the full test criteria.

Final tips for Limerick test day

Get to Castlemungret at least 15 minutes early. Parking is tight on busy test days. Have your learner permit, glasses if you need them, and your car tax and insurance disc visible. The examiner will do a vehicle check before you move off, so make sure your lights, indicators, and brake lights are all working that morning.

Nerves are normal. The learners who pass in Limerick aren't the ones who drive perfectly, they're the ones who drive the routes often enough that nothing on test day comes as a surprise.
